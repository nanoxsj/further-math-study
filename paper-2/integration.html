2.4 Integration

双曲函数积分 · 标准积分 · 递推公式 · 矩形近似 · 弧长与曲面面积

CIE 9231 Paper 2，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Collins Further Pure Mathematics 2 Chapter 4。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 求双曲函数的积分，并识别 \(\dfrac1{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\)、\(\dfrac1{x^2+a^2}\)、\(\dfrac1{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}}\) 形式的积分（必要时先完成配方）；
  2. 使用合适的三角或双曲代换求相关函数的积分；
  3. 推导并使用递推公式（reduction formulae）计算定积分；
  4. 理解曲线下面积可以用矩形面积近似，并用矩形估计面积或推导不等式与极限（矩形宽度可为 1，也可趋于 0）。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Integrating hyperbolic functions

双曲函数的积分与其导数互为逆运算：

\[ \int\cosh x\,\mathrm dx=\sinh x+C, \qquad \int\sinh x\,\mathrm dx=\cosh x+C, \]

\[ \int\tanh x\,\mathrm dx=\ln(\cosh x)+C. \]

1.2 Standard integrals and completing the square

本课程要求识别并使用以下标准积分：

\[ \int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}=\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{a}+C, \]

\[ \int\frac{\mathrm dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac1a\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{a}+C, \]

\[ \int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}}=\cosh^{-1}\frac{x}{a}+C, \]

\[ \int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}=\sinh^{-1}\frac{x}{a}+C. \]

遇到形如 \(x^2+x\)、\(3x^2+12x+15\) 的被积函数时，先配方，再化为上述标准形式。

1.3 Trigonometric and hyperbolic substitutions

  • 含 \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\)：令 \(x=a\sin\theta\)；
  • 含 \(x^2+a^2\) 或 \(\sqrt{x^2+a^2}\)：令 \(x=a\tan\theta\) 或 \(x=a\sinh u\)；
  • 含 \(\sqrt{x^2-a^2}\)：令 \(x=a\cosh u\)。

代换时注意同时更换 \(\mathrm dx\) 与积分上下限。

1.4 Reduction formulae

递推公式把含整数 \(n\) 的积分 \(I_n\) 化为 \(I_{n-1}\)、\(I_{n-2}\) 等更简单的积分。常用方法：

  • 分部积分（例如 \(I_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^nx\,\mathrm dx\)）；
  • 先对某个函数（如 \(\tan x\sec^nx\)、\(x(1+x^2)^n\)）求导，再两边积分。

例：

\[ I_n=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^nx\,\mathrm dx \;\Longrightarrow\; I_n=\frac{n-1}{n}I_{n-2}. \]

1.5 Rectangles and the area under a curve

用 \(n\) 个等宽矩形近似 \(\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm dx\)：取矩形左端点可得一个近似，取右端点可得另一个近似；若 \(f\) 单调，则二者分别是上界与下界。矩形越细，近似越精确，且当矩形宽度趋于零时，矩形面积和收敛到积分值。

1.6 Arc length and surface area

弧长：

\[ s=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dx =\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dt, \]

极坐标 \(r=f(\theta)\)：

\[ s=\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2}\sqrt{r^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm d\theta}\right)^2}\,\mathrm d\theta. \]

曲线绕 \(x\) 轴旋转一周所得曲面面积：

\[ A=2\pi\int_a^b y\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dx =2\pi\int_{t_1}^{t_2}y\sqrt{\left(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dt. \]

2. Textbook Examples

Example 1 · Page 88

Find the area enclosed by the curve \(y=\cosh x+\tanh x\), the \(y\)-axis, the \(x\)-axis and the line \(x=1\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Show solution

\[ \begin{aligned} A&=\int_0^1(\cosh x+\tanh x)\,\mathrm dx\\ &=\Bigl[\sinh x+\ln(\cosh x)\Bigr]_0^1\\ &=\sinh1+\ln(\cosh1)-0\\ &=\boxed{1.61\ (3\text{ s.f.})}. \end{aligned} \]

Example 2 · Page 89

Find \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\ln2}\operatorname{sech}x\,\mathrm dx\) correct to 3 significant figures.

Show solution

Using the definition \(\operatorname{sech}x=\dfrac2{e^x+e^{-x}}\) and the substitution \(u=e^x\), \(\mathrm du=e^x\mathrm dx\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \int\operatorname{sech}x\,\mathrm dx &=\int\frac{2}{u+u^{-1}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm du}{u} =2\int\frac{\mathrm du}{u^2+1}\\ &=2\tan^{-1}u+C =2\tan^{-1}(e^x)+C. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore

\[ \int_0^{\ln2}\operatorname{sech}x\,\mathrm dx =2\Bigl[\tan^{-1}(e^x)\Bigr]_0^{\ln2} =2\left(\tan^{-1}2-\tan^{-1}1\right) =\boxed{0.410\ (3\text{ s.f.})}. \]

Example 3 · Page 92

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{x^2+x}}\) by completing the square, for \(x>1\).

Show solution

Completing the square,

\[ x^2+x=\left(x+\frac12\right)^2-\frac14. \]

Let \(z=x+\frac12\) and \(a=\frac12\). Then

\[ \int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{x^2+x}} =\int\frac{\mathrm dz}{\sqrt{z^2-a^2}} =\cosh^{-1}\frac{z}{a}+C =\boxed{\cosh^{-1}(2x+1)+C}. \]

Example 4 · Page 92

Find \(\displaystyle\int\frac{5\,\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{3x^2+12x+15}}\) by completing the square.

Show solution

Completing the square,

\[ 3x^2+12x+15=3\bigl((x+2)^2+1\bigr). \]

Therefore

\[ \int\frac{5\,\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{3x^2+12x+15}} =\frac5{\sqrt3}\int\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{(x+2)^2+1}} =\boxed{\frac5{\sqrt3}\sinh^{-1}(x+2)+C}. \]

Example 5 · Page 94

Given that \(I_n=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^nx\,\mathrm dx\), show that

\[ I_n=\frac{n-1}{n}I_{n-2} \]

and hence evaluate \(I_5\).

Show solution

Write \(\sin^nx=\sin^{n-1}x\sin x\) and integrate by parts:

\[ \begin{aligned} I_n&=\Bigl[-\cos x\sin^{n-1}x\Bigr]_0^{\pi/2} +(n-1)\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2x\sin^{n-2}x\,\mathrm dx\\ &=0+(n-1)\int_0^{\pi/2}(1-\sin^2x)\sin^{n-2}x\,\mathrm dx\\ &=(n-1)I_{n-2}-(n-1)I_n. \end{aligned} \]

Hence

\[ nI_n=(n-1)I_{n-2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{I_n=\frac{n-1}{n}I_{n-2}}. \]

Using \(I_1=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\,\mathrm dx=1\),

\[ I_5=\frac45\cdot\frac23\cdot I_1 =\frac45\cdot\frac23 =\boxed{\frac8{15}}. \]

Example 6 · Page 95

(a) Find \(\displaystyle\int_0^1e^{-x}\,\mathrm dx\).

(b) Find a reduction formula for the integral \(\displaystyle I_n=\int_0^1e^{-x}(1-x)^n\,\mathrm dx\).

(c) Hence find \(\displaystyle\int_0^1e^{-x}(1-x)^2\,\mathrm dx\).

Show solution

(a)

\[ \int_0^1e^{-x}\,\mathrm dx=\Bigl[-e^{-x}\Bigr]_0^1=\boxed{1-\frac1e}. \]

(b) Integrating by parts with \(u=(1-x)^n\), \(\mathrm dv=e^{-x}\mathrm dx\):

\[ \begin{aligned} I_n&=\Bigl[-e^{-x}(1-x)^n\Bigr]_0^1-n\int_0^1e^{-x}(1-x)^{n-1}\,\mathrm dx\\ &=1-nI_{n-1}. \end{aligned} \]

So

\[ \boxed{I_n=1-nI_{n-1}}. \]

(c) Using the reduction formula repeatedly:

\[ I_2=1-2I_1=1-2(1-I_0)=1-2\left(1-\left(1-\frac1e\right)\right) =1-2\cdot\frac1e =\boxed{1-\frac2e}. \]

Example 7 · Page 95

Given that \(I_n=\displaystyle\int_0^1(1-x)^ne^{-x}\,\mathrm dx\), find a reduction formula for \(I_n\) by splitting \((1-x)^n\) into \((1-x)^{n-1}(1-x)\).

Show solution

Write

\[ I_n=\int_0^1(1-x)^{n-1}(1-x)e^{-x}\,\mathrm dx. \]

Using integration by parts with \(u=(1-x)^n\), \(\mathrm dv=e^{-x}\mathrm dx\),

\[ I_n=\Bigl[-e^{-x}(1-x)^n\Bigr]_0^1 -n\int_0^1e^{-x}(1-x)^{n-1}\,\mathrm dx. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{I_n=1-nI_{n-1}}. \]

Example 8 · Page 96

Find \(\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(\tan x\sec^nx\right)\) in terms of \(\sec x\). Hence find a reduction formula for \(\displaystyle I_n=\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^nx\,\mathrm dx\).

Show solution

Using the product rule,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(\tan x\sec^nx\right) =\sec^{n+2}x+n\sec^nx\tan^2x =(n+1)\sec^{n+2}x-n\sec^nx. \]

Integrating from \(0\) to \(\pi/4\),

\[ \Bigl[\tan x\sec^nx\Bigr]_0^{\pi/4} =(n+1)I_{n+2}-nI_n. \]

At \(x=\pi/4\), \(\tan x=1\) and \(\sec x=\sqrt2\), so the left-hand side is \((\sqrt2)^n=2^{n/2}\). Hence

\[ 2^{n/2}=(n+1)I_{n+2}-nI_n, \]

giving

\[ \boxed{I_{n+2}=\frac{2^{n/2}+nI_n}{n+1}}. \]

Example 9 · Page 97

Find the area under the curve \(y=1+x^2\) between \(x=1\) and \(x=2\) by using rectangles of width \(\frac15\) that underestimate the area. Find the area exactly by integrating, and give the percentage error of the approximation correct to 1 significant figure.

Show solution

Using five rectangles of width \(\frac15\) that underestimate the area (using left endpoints \(1.0,1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8\)):

\[ A\approx\frac15\Bigl[(1^2+1)+(1.2^2+1)+(1.4^2+1)+(1.6^2+1)+(1.8^2+1)\Bigr] =3.04. \]

The exact area is

\[ \int_1^2(1+x^2)\,\mathrm dx =\Bigl[x+\frac{x^3}{3}\Bigr]_1^2 =2+\frac83-1-\frac13 =\frac{10}{3}\approx3.333. \]

The percentage error is

\[ \frac{3.04-3.333}{3.333}\times100\approx\boxed{-9\%\ (1\text{ s.f.})}. \]

(Using right-endpoint rectangles gives \(3.64\), an overestimate of \(+9\%\); the average gives an estimate with error about \(+0.2\%\).)

Example 10 · Page 99

Show that

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac1{n}\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{-1}\approx\int_0^1\frac{\mathrm dx}{1+x}, \]

with the approximation improving as \(n\) increases.

Show solution

Taking \(n\) rectangles of width \(\frac1n\) under the curve \(y=\frac1{1+x}\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\) (using the right endpoints \(x=\frac rn\)):

\[ \text{sum of areas} =\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac1n\cdot\frac1{1+\frac rn}. \]

As \(n\to\infty\), the rectangles fit the curve more closely, so

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac1n\left(1+\frac rn\right)^{-1} \longrightarrow \int_0^1\frac{\mathrm dx}{1+x} =\Bigl[\ln(1+x)\Bigr]_0^1 =\ln2. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac1{n}\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{-1}\approx\ln2}, \]

with the approximation improving as \(n\) increases.

Example 11 · Page 102

Find the length of the curve given by \(x=\cos t\), \(y=\sin t\) from \(t=0\) to \(t=\pi/2\).

(a) by seeing this as an arc of a circle;

(b) by using the parametric form for arc length.

Check that both methods give the same answer.

Show solution

(a) Eliminating \(t\) gives \(x^2+y^2=1\). The required arc is the quarter-circle from \((1,0)\) to \((0,1)\), whose length is

\[ \frac14\cdot2\pi=\boxed{\frac{\pi}{2}}. \]

(b) Using

\[ s=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dt, \]

with \(\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}=-\sin t\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}=\cos t\),

\[ s=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{\sin^2t+\cos^2t}\,\mathrm dt =\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,\mathrm dt =\boxed{\frac{\pi}{2}}. \]

Both methods agree.

Example 12 · Page 103

(a) Find the derivative of \(\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)+x\sqrt{x^2+1}\) and hence find \(\displaystyle\int\sqrt{1+x^2}\,\mathrm dx\).

(b) Find the length of the spiral defined by \(r=\theta\) for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2\).

Show solution

(a) Differentiating \(\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)\) using the chain rule,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right) =\frac{1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}}{x+\sqrt{x^2+1}} =\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+1}}. \]

Differentiating \(x\sqrt{x^2+1}\) by the product rule,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(x\sqrt{x^2+1}\right) =\sqrt{x^2+1}+\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} =\frac{2x^2+1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}. \]

Adding the two derivatives,

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)+x\sqrt{x^2+1}\right) =\frac{2x^2+2}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} =2\sqrt{x^2+1}. \]

Hence

\[ \int\sqrt{1+x^2}\,\mathrm dx =\boxed{\frac12\left(\ln\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)+x\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)+C}. \]

(b) For \(r=\theta\), using the polar arc-length formula,

\[ s=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{r^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm d\theta}\right)^2}\,\mathrm d\theta =\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{\theta^2+1}\,\mathrm d\theta \approx2.30. \]

Example 13 · Page 105

Find the surface area of revolution formed by rotating the line \(y=x\) through \(2\pi\) about the \(x\)-axis from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\).

Show solution

Using

\[ A=2\pi\int_0^1 y\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dx, \]

with \(y=x\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=1\),

\[ A=2\pi\int_0^1 x\sqrt2\,\mathrm dx =2\pi\sqrt2\cdot\frac12 =\boxed{\pi\sqrt2}. \]

This is the curved surface area of a cone of slant height \(\sqrt2\) and base radius \(1\): \(A=\pi r l=\pi\sqrt2\).

Example 14 · Page 105

Find the surface area of a sphere by rotating the top half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=a^2\) through \(360^\circ\) about the \(x\)-axis.

Show solution

The top half is \(y=\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\), and

\[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}. \]

Therefore

\[ \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right)^2} =\sqrt{1+\frac{x^2}{a^2-x^2}} =\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}. \]

So

\[ A=2\pi\int_{-a}^{a}\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\cdot\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\,\mathrm dx =2\pi\int_{-a}^{a}a\,\mathrm dx =2\pi a(2a) =\boxed{4\pi a^2}. \]

This agrees with the standard formula for the surface area of a sphere.

Example 15 · Page 106

The curve \(C\) is defined by \(x=t^2\), \(y=t-\frac13t^3\). Find the surface area created when the portion of the curve from \(t=0\) to \(t=1\) is rotated through \(360^\circ\) about the \(x\)-axis.

Show solution

Using

\[ A=2\pi\int_{t_1}^{t_2}y\sqrt{\left(\frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}\right)^2}\,\mathrm dt, \]

with

\[ \frac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}=2t,\qquad \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dt}=1-t^2, \]

we have

\[ \sqrt{(2t)^2+(1-t^2)^2}=t^2+1. \]

Therefore

\[ A=2\pi\int_0^1\left(t-\frac13t^3\right)(t^2+1)\,\mathrm dt =2\pi\int_0^1\left(t^3+t-\frac13t^5-\frac13t^3\right)\mathrm dt. \]

Evaluating,

\[ A=2\pi\left[\frac{t^4}{4}+\frac{t^2}{2}-\frac{t^6}{18}-\frac{t^4}{12}\right]_0^1 =2\pi\left(\frac14+\frac12-\frac1{18}-\frac1{12}\right). \]

So

\[ A=2\pi\left(\frac{9+18-2-3}{36}\right) =2\pi\cdot\frac{22}{36} =\boxed{\frac{11\pi}{9}}. \]

The diagram shows the curve with equation \(y=x^2\) for \(0\leqslant x\leqslant1\), together with a set of \(n\) rectangles of width \(\dfrac1n\).

(a) By considering the sum of the areas of these rectangles, show that

\[ \int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx<\frac{2n^2+3n+1}{6n^2}. \]

[4]

(b) Use a similar method to find, in terms of \(n\), a lower bound for \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx\). [4]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Using the rectangles that overestimate the area (right endpoints \(\frac1n,\frac2n,\dots,1\)),

\[ \int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx <\frac1n\left(\left(\frac1n\right)^2+\left(\frac2n\right)^2+\cdots+\left(\frac nn\right)^2\right) =\frac1{n^3}\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^2. \]

Using \(\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^nr^2=\frac16n(n+1)(2n+1)\),

\[ \int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx <\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^3} =\boxed{\frac{2n^2+3n+1}{6n^2}}. \]

(b) Using the rectangles that underestimate the area (left endpoints \(0,\frac1n,\dots,\frac{n-1}{n}\)),

\[ \int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx >\frac1n\left(0+\left(\frac1n\right)^2+\cdots+\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^2\right) =\frac1{n^3}\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}r^2. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{\int_0^1x^2\,\mathrm dx>\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6n^3}=\frac{(n-1)(2n-1)}{6n^2}}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/M/J/2020, Q4; 9231/22/M/J/2020, Q4.

2020 May/June · 9231/21 & 9231/22 · Question 5 · 11 marks

The curves \(C_1:y=\cosh x\) and \(C_2:y=\sinh 2x\) intersect at the point where \(x=a\).

(a) Find the exact value of \(a\), giving your answer in logarithmic form. [4]

(b) Sketch \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) on the same diagram. [2]

(c) Find the exact value of the length of the arc of \(C_1\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=a\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) At the intersection, \(\cosh a=\sinh 2a=2\sinh a\cosh a\), so \(\sinh a=\frac12\). Hence

\[ a=\sinh^{-1}\frac12=\ln\left(\frac12+\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\right). \]

(b) \(C_1\) is the even U-shaped curve \(y=\cosh x\); \(C_2\) is the odd increasing curve \(y=\sinh 2x\), intersecting \(C_1\) in the first quadrant at \(x=a\).

(c) For \(C_1:y=\cosh x\),

\[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}=\sinh x, \qquad \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm dx}\right)^2}=\cosh x. \]

Hence

\[ s=\int_0^a\cosh x\,\mathrm dx =\sinh a =\boxed{\frac12}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/M/J/2020, Q5; 9231/22/M/J/2020, Q5.

2020 May/June · 9231/21 & 9231/22 · Question 6 · 10 marks

The integral \(I_n\), where \(n\) is an integer, is defined by

\[ I_n=\int_0^{1/2}\left(1-x^2\right)^{-\frac12 n}\mathrm dx. \]

(a) Find the exact value of \(I_1\). [2]

(b) By considering \(\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\left(x(1-x^2)^{-\frac12 n}\right)\), or otherwise, show that

\[ nI_{n+2}=2^{n-1}3^{-\frac12 n}+(n-1)I_n. \]

[5]

(c) Find the exact value of \(I_5\). [3]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a)

\[ I_1=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{\mathrm dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} =\Bigl[\sin^{-1}x\Bigr]_0^{1/2} =\boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}}. \]

(b) Differentiating,
