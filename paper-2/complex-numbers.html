2.5 Complex numbers

de Moivre 定理 · 单位根 · 倍角公式 · C+iS 求和

CIE 9231 Paper 2，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Collins Further Pure Mathematics 2 Chapter 5。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 理解 de Moivre 定理对正整数或负整数指数的含义，并与复数乘除法的几何效果联系；
  2. 用数学归纳法证明正整数指数的 de Moivre 定理；
  3. 使用正整数或负有理数指数的 de Moivre 定理：
    • 把倍角的三角比表示成基本角三角比的幂（如 \(\cos5\theta\) 用 \(\cos\theta\) 表示，\(\tan5\theta\) 用 \(\tan\theta\) 表示）；
    • 把 \(\sin\) 与 \(\cos\) 的幂表示成倍角形式（如 \(\sin^6\theta\) 用 \(\cos2\theta,\cos4\theta,\cos6\theta\) 表示）；
    • 用 C+iS 方法求和（如 \(\sum \sin r\theta\)）；
    • 求并利用 \(n\) 次单位根。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 De Moivre’s theorem

若 \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\)，则

\[ \boxed{(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)^n=\cos n\theta+\mathrm i\sin n\theta}. \]

对正整数 \(n\) 可用数学归纳法证明；结果对负整数和有理数 \(n\) 也成立。几何上，\(z\) 的幂均匀分布在单位圆上，模相乘、辐角相加。

1.2 The nth roots of unity

\(z^n=1\) 的 \(n\) 个根为

\[ z=e^{\mathrm i 2k\pi/n},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,n-1, \]

它们在单位圆上均匀分布，且所有根都可以写成某个原根的幂。非 1 的单位根满足

\[ 1+z+z^2+\cdots+z^{n-1}=0. \]

一般复数方程 \(z^n=a\) 先写 \(a=r(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)\)，再取

\[ z=r^{1/n}\left(\cos\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}\right). \]

1.3 Trigonometric ratios of multiple angles

设 \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\)，则

\[ z+z^{-1}=2\cos\theta,\qquad z-z^{-1}=2\mathrm i\sin\theta, \]

更一般地，

\[ z^n+z^{-n}=2\cos n\theta,\qquad z^n-z^{-n}=2\mathrm i\sin n\theta. \]

展开 \((z+z^{-1})^n\) 或 \((z-z^{-1})^n\) 并取实部或虚部，可以得到倍角公式。例如

\[ \cos3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta, \]

\[ \sin5\theta=16\sin^5\theta-20\sin^3\theta+5\sin\theta. \]

1.4 Powers of trigonometric ratios

反过来，用上述方法可以把 \(\sin^n\theta\)、\(\cos^n\theta\) 写成倍角项的线性组合，例如

\[ \sin^3\theta=\frac14(3\sin\theta-\sin3\theta), \]

\[ \cos^5\theta=\frac1{16}\cos5\theta+\frac5{16}\cos3\theta+\frac58\cos\theta. \]

这些形式特别适合直接积分。

1.5 Finite series of trigonometric ratios (C+iS method)

令 \(C=\sum\cos r\theta\)、\(S=\sum\sin r\theta\)，则

\[ C+\mathrm iS=\sum z^r \]

是等比级数，可以直接求和。取实部或虚部即得原三角级数。常用

\[ z-1=2\mathrm i z^{1/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2},\qquad z+1=2z^{1/2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}. \]

例如

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\cos r\theta=\frac{\sin\frac{n\theta}{2}\cos\frac{(n+1)\theta}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}. \]

2. Textbook Examples

Example 1 · Page 104

\(z=\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\). Find the exact value of:

(a) \(z^6\);

(b) \(\dfrac1{z^2}\).

Show solution

Using de Moivre’s theorem, \((\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)^n=\cos n\theta+\mathrm i\sin n\theta\).

(a)

\[ z^6=\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^6 =\cos\frac{6\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{6\pi}{4} =\cos\frac{3\pi}{2}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{3\pi}{2} =\boxed{-\mathrm i}. \]

(b)

\[ \frac1{z^2}=z^{-2} =\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^{-2} =\cos\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\mathrm i\sin\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right) =\boxed{-\mathrm i}. \]

Example 2 · Page 108

(a) Find the eighth roots of unity.

(b) Show that they can all be written as powers of \(\dfrac{\mathrm i-1}{\sqrt2}\).

Show solution

(a) The roots are equally spaced around the unit circle. The angle between adjacent roots is

\[ \frac{2\pi}{8}=\frac{\pi}{4}. \]

Four of the roots are \(1\), \(-1\), \(\mathrm i\) and \(-\mathrm i\). The other four are

\[ \pm\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\right) =\pm\frac{1+\mathrm i}{\sqrt2}, \]

and

\[ \pm\left(\cos\frac{3\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) =\pm\frac{-1+\mathrm i}{\sqrt2}. \]

So the eighth roots of unity are

\[ \boxed{\pm1,\ \pm\mathrm i,\ \pm\frac{1+\mathrm i}{\sqrt2},\ \pm\frac{-1+\mathrm i}{\sqrt2}}. \]

(b) Let

\[ w=\frac{\mathrm i-1}{\sqrt2}=\cos\frac{3\pi}{4}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}. \]

Then \(w=e^{\mathrm i3\pi/4}\), so the powers \(w^0,w^1,w^2,\dots,w^7\) are

\[ 1,\ w,\ w^2,\ w^3,\ w^4,\ w^5,\ w^6,\ w^7, \]

which are the points \(e^{\mathrm i 3k\pi/4}\) for \(k=0,1,\dots,7\). These are exactly the eight equally spaced roots found in part (a).

Example 3 · Page 109

(a) Find the cube roots of \(8\).

(b) Find the cube roots of \(8\mathrm i\).

Show solution

(a) Let \(z=r(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)\). Then \(z^3=r^3(\cos3\theta+\mathrm i\sin3\theta)=8\). Hence \(r^3=8\), so \(r=2\), and \(\cos3\theta=1\), \(\sin3\theta=0\), so \(3\theta\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\):

\[ 3\theta=0,\ 2\pi,\ 4\pi,\ 6\pi,\dots \]

The three distinct values of \(\theta\) are \(0\), \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\) and \(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\). Hence the cube roots of \(8\) are

\[ \boxed{2,\quad 2\left(\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}\right),\quad 2\left(\cos\frac{4\pi}{3}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)}. \]

(b) Now \(z^3=8\mathrm i\), so \(r=2\) and

\[ \cos3\theta+\mathrm i\sin3\theta=\mathrm i=\cos\frac{\pi}{2}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Thus \(3\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}+2k\pi\), giving \(\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{6},\dfrac{5\pi}{6},\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\). The cube roots of \(8\mathrm i\) are

\[ \boxed{ 2\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{6}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\right),\quad 2\left(\cos\frac{5\pi}{6}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{5\pi}{6}\right),\quad 2\left(\cos\frac{3\pi}{2}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)=-2\mathrm i }. \]

Example 4 · Page 111

(a) Use de Moivre’s theorem to prove that

\[ \cos3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta. \]

(b) Solve the equation \(\cos3\theta+\cos\theta=0\) for \(0\leqslant\theta<2\pi\).

Show solution

(a) By de Moivre’s theorem,

\[ (\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)^3=\cos3\theta+\mathrm i\sin3\theta. \]

Expanding the left-hand side,

\[ (\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)^3 =\cos^3\theta+3\mathrm i\cos^2\theta\sin\theta-3\cos\theta\sin^2\theta-\mathrm i\sin^3\theta. \]

Taking real parts,

\[ \cos3\theta=\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta\sin^2\theta. \]

Using \(\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta\),

\[ \cos3\theta=\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta(1-\cos^2\theta) =\boxed{4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta}. \]

(b) Using part (a),

\[ \cos3\theta+\cos\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta+\cos\theta =2\cos\theta(2\cos^2\theta-1)=0. \]

So \(\cos\theta=0\) or \(\cos^2\theta=\dfrac12\). Hence

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{\pi}{2},\ \frac{3\pi}{2},\ \frac{\pi}{4},\ \frac{3\pi}{4},\ \frac{5\pi}{4},\ \frac{7\pi}{4}}. \]

Example 5 · Page 113

(a) Show that

\[ \sin^3\theta=\frac14(3\sin\theta-\sin3\theta). \]

(b) Hence find \(\displaystyle\int\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\).

Show solution

(a) Let \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\). Then

\[ z-z^{-1}=2\mathrm i\sin\theta. \]

Cubing,

\[ (2\mathrm i\sin\theta)^3=(z-z^{-1})^3 =z^3-3z+3z^{-1}-z^{-3}. \]

Using \(z^n+z^{-n}=2\cos n\theta\) and \(z^n-z^{-n}=2\mathrm i\sin n\theta\),

\[ (z-z^{-1})^3=(z^3-z^{-3})-3(z-z^{-1}) =2\mathrm i\sin3\theta-6\mathrm i\sin\theta. \]

Hence

\[ -8\mathrm i\sin^3\theta=2\mathrm i\sin3\theta-6\mathrm i\sin\theta, \]

so

\[ \boxed{\sin^3\theta=\frac14(3\sin\theta-\sin3\theta)}. \]

(b)

\[ \int\sin^3\theta\,\mathrm d\theta =\frac14\int(3\sin\theta-\sin3\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta =\boxed{-\frac34\cos\theta+\frac1{12}\cos3\theta+C}. \]

Example 6 · Page 117

Show that

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{4}\cos r\theta=\frac{\sin2\theta\,\cos\frac{5\theta}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}. \]

Show solution

Let \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\) and write

\[ C+\mathrm iS=z+z^2+z^3+z^4. \]

Then the real part \(C=\sum_{r=1}^4\cos r\theta\). The sum is a geometric series:

\[ z+z^2+z^3+z^4=\frac{z(z^4-1)}{z-1}. \]

Now

\[ z^4-1=\cos4\theta+\mathrm i\sin4\theta-1 =-2\sin^22\theta+2\mathrm i\sin2\theta\cos2\theta =2\mathrm i\sin2\theta(\cos2\theta+\mathrm i\sin2\theta) =2\mathrm i z^2\sin2\theta, \]

and

\[ z-1=\cos\theta-1+\mathrm i\sin\theta =-2\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}+2\mathrm i\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2} =2\mathrm i\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+\mathrm i\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\right) =2\mathrm i z^{1/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}. \]

Therefore

\[ C+\mathrm iS =\frac{z\cdot2\mathrm i z^2\sin2\theta}{2\mathrm i z^{1/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}} =z^{5/2}\frac{\sin2\theta}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}. \]

Taking real parts,

\[ \boxed{\sum_{r=1}^{4}\cos r\theta =\frac{\sin2\theta\,\cos\frac{5\theta}{2}}{\sin\frac{\theta}{2}}}. \]

This is the \(C+\mathrm iS\) method.

2020 May/June · 9231/23 · Question 8 · 15 marks

(a) Use de Moivre’s theorem to show that

\[ \sin^6\theta=\frac1{32}\left(\cos6\theta-6\cos4\theta+15\cos2\theta-10\right). \]

[6]

It is given that

\[ \cos^6\theta=\frac1{32}\left(\cos6\theta+6\cos4\theta+15\cos2\theta+10\right). \]

(b) Find the exact value of \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/3}\left(\cos^6\frac{x}{4}+\sin^6\frac{x}{4}\right)\mathrm dx\). [4]

(c) Express each root of the equation

\[ 16c^6+16(1-c^2)^3-13=0 \]

in the form \(\cos k\pi\), where \(k\) is a rational number. [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Using \((z-z^{-1})^6\) with \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\),

\[ (z-z^{-1})^6=(z^6+z^{-6})-6(z^4+z^{-4})+15(z^2+z^{-2})-20. \]

Since \(z-z^{-1}=2\mathrm i\sin\theta\) and \(z^n+z^{-n}=2\cos n\theta\),

\[ (2\mathrm i\sin\theta)^6=2\cos6\theta-12\cos4\theta+30\cos2\theta-20. \]

Hence

\[ 64\sin^6\theta=2\cos6\theta-12\cos4\theta+30\cos2\theta-20, \]

so

\[ \sin^6\theta=\frac1{32}\left(\cos6\theta-6\cos4\theta+15\cos2\theta-10\right). \]

(b) Adding the given expression for \(\cos^6\frac{x}{4}\),

\[ \cos^6\frac{x}{4}+\sin^6\frac{x}{4} =\frac1{32}\left(2\cos\frac{3x}{2}+30\right) =\frac18\left(\cos\frac{3x}{2}+15\right). \]

Therefore

\[ \int_0^{\pi/3}\left(\cos^6\frac{x}{4}+\sin^6\frac{x}{4}\right)\mathrm dx =\frac18\left[\frac23\sin\frac{3x}{2}+15x\right]_0^{\pi/3} =\boxed{\frac18\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}+\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right)}. \]

(c) Let \(c=\cos\theta\), so \(1-c^2=\sin^2\theta\). Then

\[ 16\cos^6\theta+16\sin^6\theta-13=0. \]

Using the identities from parts (a) and the given expression,

\[ 16\cdot\frac1{32}(\cos6\theta+6\cos4\theta+15\cos2\theta+10) +16\cdot\frac1{32}(\cos6\theta-6\cos4\theta+15\cos2\theta-10)-13=0. \]

Simplifying,

\[ \cos6\theta+15\cos2\theta-13=0. \]

Using \(\cos6\theta=4\cos^32\theta-3\cos2\theta\) (or directly), this reduces to

\[ 6\cos4\theta-3=0, \]

so \(\cos4\theta=\dfrac12\), giving \(4\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{3},\dfrac{5\pi}{3},\dfrac{7\pi}{3},\dfrac{11\pi}{3}\). Hence

\[ \boxed{c=\cos\frac{\pi}{12},\ \cos\frac{5\pi}{12},\ \cos\frac{7\pi}{12},\ \cos\frac{11\pi}{12}}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/23/M/J/2020, Q8.

2020 October/November · 9231/21 & 9231/23 · Question 6 · 11 marks

(a) Use de Moivre’s theorem to show that

\[ \sin^4\theta=\frac18\left(\cos4\theta-4\cos2\theta+3\right). \]

[5]

(b) Find the solution of the differential equation

\[ \frac{\mathrm dy}{\mathrm d\theta}+y\cot\theta=\sin^3\theta \]

for which \(y=0\) when \(\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\). [6]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Using \((z-z^{-1})^4\) with \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\),

\[ (z-z^{-1})^4=(z^4+z^{-4})-4(z^2+z^{-2})+6. \]

Since \(z-z^{-1}=2\mathrm i\sin\theta\) and \(z^n+z^{-n}=2\cos n\theta\),

\[ 16\sin^4\theta=2\cos4\theta-8\cos2\theta+6, \]

so

\[ \sin^4\theta=\frac18(\cos4\theta-4\cos2\theta+3). \]

(b) The integrating factor is

\[ e^{\int\cot\theta\,\mathrm d\theta}=e^{\ln\sin\theta}=\sin\theta. \]

Hence

\[ \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\theta}(y\sin\theta)=\sin^4\theta =\frac18(\cos4\theta-4\cos2\theta+3). \]

Integrating,

\[ y\sin\theta=\frac18\left(\frac14\sin4\theta-2\sin2\theta+3\theta\right)+C. \]

Using \(y=0\) at \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\),

\[ 0=\frac18\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)+C \;\Longrightarrow\; C=-\frac{3\pi}{16}. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{y\sin\theta=\frac18\left(\frac14\sin4\theta-2\sin2\theta+3\theta-\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/O/N/2020, Q6; 9231/23/O/N/2020, Q6.

2020 October/November · 9231/22 · Question 7 · 7 marks

(a) Show that

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}z^{2r}=\frac{z^{2n+1}-z}{z^2-1}, \]

for \(z\neq0,1,-1\). [2]

(b) By letting \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\), show that, if \(\sin\theta\neq0\),

\[ 1+2\sum_{r=1}^{n}\cos(2r\theta)=\frac{\sin(2n+1)\theta}{\sin\theta}. \]

[5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The sum is a geometric series with first term \(z^2\) and common ratio \(z^2\):

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}z^{2r} =z^2+z^4+\cdots+z^{2n} =\frac{z^2(z^{2n}-1)}{z^2-1} =\boxed{\frac{z^{2n+1}-z}{z^2-1}}. \]

(b) With \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\), by de Moivre’s theorem \(z^{2n+1}=\cos(2n+1)\theta+\mathrm i\sin(2n+1)\theta\), and

\[ z^2-1=(\cos2\theta-1)+\mathrm i\sin2\theta =-2\sin^2\theta+2\mathrm i\sin\theta\cos\theta =2\mathrm i\sin\theta(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta) =2\mathrm i z\sin\theta. \]

Also \(z^{-1}=\cos\theta-\mathrm i\sin\theta\), so

\[ \frac{z^{2n+1}-z}{z^2-1} =\frac{z^{2n+1}-z}{2\mathrm i z\sin\theta}. \]

Writing \(z^{2r}=z^2(z^2)^{r-1}\) and using the result of part (a) on \(\sum z^{2r}\), taking real parts gives

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\cos(2r\theta) =\frac{\sin(2n+1)\theta}{2\sin\theta}-\frac12. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{1+2\sum_{r=1}^{n}\cos(2r\theta)=\frac{\sin(2n+1)\theta}{\sin\theta}}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/22/O/N/2020, Q7.

2021 May/June · 9231/21 & 9231/22 · Question 5 · 10 marks

(a) State the sum of the series \(z+z^2+z^3+\cdots+z^n\), for \(z\neq1\). [1]

(b) Given that \(z\) is an \(n\)th root of unity and \(z\neq1\), deduce that

\[ 1+z+z^2+\cdots+z^{n-1}=0. \]

[2]

(c) Given instead that \(z=\frac13(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)\), use de Moivre’s theorem to show that

\[ \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}3^{-m}\cos m\theta=\frac{3\cos\theta-1}{10-6\cos\theta}. \]

[7]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) For a geometric series,

\[ \boxed{z+z^2+\cdots+z^n=\frac{z-z^{n+1}}{1-z}}. \]

(b) If \(z^n=1\) and \(z\neq1\), then using part (a) with \(n\) replaced by \(n-1\),

\[ 1+z+\cdots+z^{n-1}=\frac{1-z^n}{1-z}=0. \]

(c) With \(z=\frac13(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)\), \(|z|=\frac13<1\), so

\[ \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}z^m=\frac{z}{1-z} =\frac{\frac13(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)}{1-\frac13(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)}. \]

Multiplying numerator and denominator by \(3\) and rationalising,

\[ \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}z^m =\frac{\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta}{3-\cos\theta-\mathrm i\sin\theta}. \]

Multiplying by the conjugate of the denominator,

\[ \frac{(\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)(3-\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta)}{(3-\cos\theta)^2+\sin^2\theta}. \]

The real part is

\[ \frac{3\cos\theta-\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta}{9-6\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta} =\frac{3\cos\theta-1}{10-6\cos\theta}. \]

Since \(\sum 3^{-m}\cos m\theta\) is the real part of \(\sum z^m\),

\[ \boxed{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}3^{-m}\cos m\theta=\frac{3\cos\theta-1}{10-6\cos\theta}}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/M/J/2021, Q5; 9231/22/M/J/2021, Q5.

2021 May/June · 9231/23 · Question 4 · 7 marks

By considering the binomial expansions of \(\left(z+\frac1z\right)^5\) and \(\left(z-\frac1z\right)^5\), where \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\), use de Moivre’s theorem to show that

\[ \tan5\theta=\frac{\sin5\theta-a\sin3\theta+b\sin\theta}{\cos5\theta+a\cos3\theta+b\cos\theta}, \]

where \(a\) and \(b\) are integers to be determined.

Show answer and mark scheme

Expanding \(\left(z+\frac1z\right)^5\) and \(\left(z-\frac1z\right)^5\),

\[ \left(z+\frac1z\right)^5=(z^5+z^{-5})+5(z^3+z^{-3})+10(z+z^{-1}), \]

\[ \left(z-\frac1z\right)^5=(z^5-z^{-5})-5(z^3-z^{-3})+10(z-z^{-1}). \]

With \(z=\cos\theta+\mathrm i\sin\theta\), \(z^n+z^{-n}=2\cos n\theta\) and \(z^n-z^{-n}=2\mathrm i\sin n\theta\). Hence

\[ 32\cos^5\theta=2\cos5\theta+10\cos3\theta+20\cos\theta, \]

so

\[ 16\cos^5\theta=\cos5\theta+5\cos3\theta+10\cos\theta, \]

and

\[ 32\mathrm i\sin^5\theta=2\mathrm i\sin5\theta-10\mathrm i\sin3\theta+20\mathrm i\sin\theta, \]

so

\[ 16\sin^5\theta=\sin5\theta-5\sin3\theta+10\sin\theta. \]

Therefore
