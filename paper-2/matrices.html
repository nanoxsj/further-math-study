2.2 Matrices

线性方程组 · 特征值特征向量 · 对角化 · 特征方程

CIE 9231 Paper 2，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Collins Further Pure Mathematics 2 Chapter 2。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 把三个未知数、三个线性方程组的求解问题写成矩阵方程，或反过来把矩阵方程写成方程组；
  2. 理解方程组一致与不一致的各种情形，联系系数矩阵的 singular/non-singular 性质，解一致方程组，并从直线和平面的角度作出几何解释；
  3. 理解 square matrix 的 characteristic equation、eigenvalue 与 eigenvector 的含义，并用定义 \(A\mathbf e=\lambda\mathbf e\) 证明简单性质（例如 \(\lambda^n\) 是 \(A^n\) 的特征值）；
  4. 求 \(2\times2\) 与 \(3\times3\) 矩阵的特征值和特征向量（只要求实且互异的特征值）；
  5. 把方阵写成 \(QDQ^{-1}\)，其中 \(D\) 是以特征值为对角元的对角矩阵，\(Q\) 的列是对应特征向量，并利用该形式计算矩阵幂；
  6. 使用方阵满足自身特征方程这一事实（仅限 \(2\times2\)、\(3\times3\) 矩阵）。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Solving simultaneous linear equations: with unique solutions

三个未知数、三个方程的线性方程组

\[ a_1x+b_1y+c_1z=d_1,\quad a_2x+b_2y+c_2z=d_2,\quad a_3x+b_3y+c_3z=d_3 \]

可以写成矩阵方程

\[ M\mathbf x=\mathbf b, \]

其中 \(M\) 是系数矩阵，\(\mathbf x=(x,y,z)^{\mathsf T}\)。若 \(\det M\ne0\)，则 \(M\) non-singular，存在唯一解

\[ \mathbf x=M^{-1}\mathbf b. \]

求 \(3\times3\) 逆矩阵时，先求余子式（co-factors）、代数余子式矩阵及其转置（adjugate matrix），再除以 \(\det M\)。

1.2 Geometrical interpretation: three planes

三个方程对应三个平面，解的几何情形由 \(\det M\) 与平面之间的位置关系决定：

  • \(\det M\ne0\)：三平面交于唯一一点；
  • \(\det M=0\) 且有解：三平面共线交于一条直线（sheaf of planes），或三平面重合；
  • \(\det M=0\) 且无解：如三平面构成 triangular prism，或存在平行平面。

判别方法：先看 \(\det M\)；再看有无平面平行（法向量成比例）；若平行则检查是否相同；最后检查能否由两个平面的线性组合得到第三个（左右两边同时成立则为 sheaf，仅系数成立则为 prism）。

1.3 Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

若 \(M\mathbf e=\lambda\mathbf e\) 且 \(\mathbf e\ne\mathbf0\)，则 \(\lambda\) 是 \(M\) 的 eigenvalue，\(\mathbf e\) 是对应的 eigenvector。几何上，\(M\) 把方向 \(\mathbf e\) 上的向量放大 \(\lambda\) 倍（invariant line 上的缩放）。

特征方程为

\[ \boxed{\det(M-\lambda I)=0}. \]

特征值 \(\lambda\) 的全体非零特征向量（连同零向量）构成对应特征子空间。若 \(M\mathbf e=\lambda\mathbf e\)，则

\[ M^2\mathbf e=\lambda^2\mathbf e,\qquad M^n\mathbf e=\lambda^n\mathbf e. \]

1.4 Diagonalisation and powers of matrices

若 \(3\times3\) 矩阵 \(M\) 有互异的实特征值 \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) 与对应特征向量 \(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2,\mathbf e_3\)，令

\[ Q=\bigl(\mathbf e_1\ \mathbf e_2\ \mathbf e_3\bigr), \qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3), \]

则 \(Q\) non-singular 且

\[ \boxed{M=QDQ^{-1}}. \]

于是

\[ \boxed{M^n=QD^nQ^{-1}}. \]

计算矩阵幂时，只需把每个特征值取 \(n\) 次幂，再乘回 \(Q\) 与 \(Q^{-1}\)。

1.5 The characteristic equation

Cayley–Hamilton 定理（本课程范围内）：每个方阵满足自己的特征方程。若特征方程为

\[ \lambda^3+p\lambda^2+q\lambda+r=0, \]

则

\[ M^3+pM^2+qM+rI=O. \]

这一结果可用来化简 \(M\) 的高次幂；若 \(r\ne0\)，两边左乘 \(M^{-1}\) 还可以把 \(M^{-1}\) 表示成 \(M^2\)、\(M\) 与 \(I\) 的线性组合。

2. Textbook Examples

Example 1 · Page 21

You invest a total of \(\$22000\) in three different funds:

  • Fund \(x\) offers \(4\%\) interest per year with low risk.
  • Fund \(y\) offers \(5\%\) interest per year with medium risk.
  • Fund \(z\) offers \(10\%\) interest per year with high risk.
  • Fund \(z\) appears more promising than fund \(y\), so you invest three times more with \(z\) than with \(y\).

In the first year you earn a total of \(\$1450\) in interest, but you forget how much you invested in \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\). Work out what your initial investments were.

Show solution

The information can be written as

\[ x+y+z=22000,\qquad 0.04x+0.05y+0.1z=1450,\qquad 3y=z, \]

or

\[ 4x+5y+10z=145000,\qquad 3y-z=0. \]

In matrix form,

\[ \begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\4&5&10\\0&3&-1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}22000\\145000\\0\end{pmatrix}. \]

The inverse of the coefficient matrix is

\[ M^{-1}=-\frac1{19} \begin{pmatrix}-35&4&5\\4&-1&-6\\12&-3&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Hence

\[ \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix} =-\frac1{19} \begin{pmatrix}-35&4&5\\4&-1&-6\\12&-3&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}22000\\145000\\0\end{pmatrix} =\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}8000\\3000\\9000\end{pmatrix}}. \]

So the initial investments were \(\$8000\) in \(x\), \(\$3000\) in \(y\) and \(\$9000\) in \(z\).

Example 2 · Page 27

Determine the nature of the intersection of the following planes. If they have a solution, state clearly the solution as a single point or the equation of the line or the equation of the plane of intersection.

\[ \Pi_1:-x+2y=8,\qquad \Pi_2:2x+3y-z=23,\qquad \Pi_3:-x+4y-2z=26. \]

Show solution

Write the left-hand side as a matrix:

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&0\\2&3&-1\\-1&4&-2\end{pmatrix}. \]

Calculating the determinant using the first row,

\[ \det M=-1\bigl(3(-2)-(-1)(4)\bigr)-2\bigl(2(-2)-(-1)(-1)\bigr)+0=12. \]

As \(\det M\ne0\), the planes meet at a unique point. Solving

\[ M^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}8\\23\\26\end{pmatrix} \]

gives

\[ \boxed{x=2,\quad y=5,\quad z=-4}. \]

Example 3 · Page 28

Determine whether or not the following planes have any solution, and state the configuration of the planes.

\[ \Pi_1:3x+3y+4z=6,\qquad \Pi_2:x+z=1,\qquad \Pi_3:-6x+3y-5z=-3. \]

Show solution

This can be written as

\[ \begin{pmatrix}3&3&4\\1&0&1\\-6&3&-5\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}6\\1\\-3\end{pmatrix}. \]

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

\[ \det M=3\bigl(0(-5)-1(3)\bigr)-0+3\bigl(1(3)-1(4)\bigr)=0. \]

So there is not a unique solution. None of the planes is parallel (no left-hand sides are scalar multiples), so the configuration is either a triangular prism or a sheaf.

By inspecting the equations, \(7\Pi_1+\Pi_3=\Pi_2\) for both the left-hand sides and the right-hand sides:

\[ 7(3x+3y+4z)+(-6x+3y-5z)=15x+24y+23z, \]

and

\[ 7(6)+(-3)=39. \]

This confirms the planes form a sheaf. Let \(z=\lambda\). From \(\Pi_2\), \(x=1-\lambda\). Substituting into \(\Pi_1\),

\[ 3(1-\lambda)+3y+4\lambda=6 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=1-\frac{\lambda}3. \]

Hence the line of intersection is

\[ \boxed{\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix} +\lambda\begin{pmatrix}-1\\-\frac13\\1\end{pmatrix}} \]

or, equivalently,

\[ \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix} +\mu\begin{pmatrix}-3\\-1\\3\end{pmatrix}. \]

Example 4 · Page 35

A village has a population of 700. Every person belongs to one of two competing fitness clubs. Each month, Hardrock Gym keeps \(50\%\) of its members but loses the other \(50\%\) to its rival; Pure Gym keeps \(80\%\) of its members but loses the other \(20\%\) to its rival. At the start of January the clubs have 350 members each.

(a) Show that the matrix calculation for finding the following month’s membership is

\[ \begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.2\\0.5&0.8\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}350\\350\end{pmatrix}, \]

and explain what the numbers represent.

(b) How many members does each gym have at the start of February?

(c) Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors for \(\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.2\\0.5&0.8\end{pmatrix}\).

(d) Which eigenvector represents the long-term membership? What is the stable number of members for Hardrock Gym and Pure Gym?

Show solution

(a) The first row gives Hardrock’s members: they keep \(0.5\times350\) of their own and gain \(0.2\times350\) from Pure. The second row gives Pure’s members: they gain \(0.5\times350\) and keep \(0.8\times350\).

(b)

\[ \begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.2\\0.5&0.8\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}350\\350\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}245\\455\end{pmatrix}. \]

So Hardrock has \(\boxed{245}\) members and Pure has \(\boxed{455}\).

(c) The characteristic equation is

\[ \det\begin{pmatrix}0.5-\lambda&0.2\\0.5&0.8-\lambda\end{pmatrix}=0, \]

giving

\[ \lambda^2-1.3\lambda+0.3=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (\lambda-0.3)(\lambda-1)=0, \]

so

\[ \lambda=0.3\ \text{or}\ 1. \]

For \(\lambda=0.3\), \((M-0.3I)\mathbf e=0\) gives \(y=-x\), so an eigenvector is

\[ \mathbf e_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(\lambda=1\), \((M-I)\mathbf e=0\) gives \(y=\frac52x\), so an eigenvector is

\[ \mathbf e_2=\begin{pmatrix}2\\5\end{pmatrix}. \]

(d) The eigenvector \(\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}\) does not make sense in terms of members, so the long-term membership is described by \(\begin{pmatrix}2\\5\end{pmatrix}\) with eigenvalue \(1\). Since the total is 700,

\[ 2k+5k=700\;\Longrightarrow\;k=100. \]

So the stable membership is

\[ \boxed{200\ \text{members for Hardrock Gym and}\ 500\ \text{for Pure Gym}}. \]

Example 5 · Page 38

Find the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the matrix

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}-2&6&-6\\-11&13&2\\-17&6&9\end{pmatrix}. \]

Show solution

The characteristic equation is \(\det(M-\lambda I)=0\):

\[ \begin{vmatrix}-2-\lambda&6&-6\\-11&13-\lambda&2\\-17&6&9-\lambda\end{vmatrix}=0. \]

Expanding gives

\[ \lambda^3-20\lambda^2+25\lambda+750=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (\lambda+5)(\lambda-10)(\lambda-15)=0. \]

So the eigenvalues are

\[ \boxed{\lambda=-5,\ 10,\ 15}. \]

For \(\lambda=-5\), solving \((M+5I)\mathbf e=0\) gives

\[ \mathbf e=\begin{pmatrix}1\\\frac12\\1\end{pmatrix} \sim\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\2\end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(\lambda=10\), solving \((M-10I)\mathbf e=0\) gives

\[ \mathbf e=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\3\\1\end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(\lambda=15\), solving \((M-15I)\mathbf e=0\) gives

\[ \mathbf e=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Example 6 · Page 40

Show that if \(M\) has an eigenvector \(\mathbf e\) with an eigenvalue \(\lambda\), then \(M^2\) has an eigenvalue \(\lambda^2\).

Show solution

The properties of eigenvalues and eigenvectors give

\[ M\mathbf e=\lambda\mathbf e. \]

Then

\[ M^2\mathbf e=M(M\mathbf e)=M(\lambda\mathbf e)=\lambda(M\mathbf e)=\lambda^2\mathbf e. \]

So \(\lambda^2\) is an eigenvalue of \(M^2\), with the same eigenvector \(\mathbf e\).

Example 7 · Page 45

Given the matrix

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}-3&2&-2\\-3&2&-15\\1&-1&15\end{pmatrix}, \]

(a) find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of \(A\);

(b) find a non-singular matrix \(Q\) and a diagonal matrix \(D\) such that \(A=QDQ^{-1}\).

Show solution

(a) The characteristic equation is

\[ \begin{vmatrix}-3-\lambda&2&-2\\-3&2-\lambda&-15\\1&-1&15-\lambda\end{vmatrix}=0, \]

which expands to

\[ 2\lambda^3-\lambda^2-2\lambda+1=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)(2\lambda-1)=0. \]

So

\[ \boxed{\lambda=-1,\ \tfrac12,\ 1}. \]

For \(\lambda=-1\), \((A+I)\mathbf e=0\) gives \(x=y\), so

\[ \mathbf e_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(\lambda=\frac12\), \((A-\frac12 I)\mathbf e=0\) gives \(y=z\), so

\[ \mathbf e_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(\lambda=1\), \((A-I)\mathbf e=0\) gives \(z=-2x\), so

\[ \mathbf e_3=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Taking the eigenvectors as columns,

\[ Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\1&1&0\\0&1&-2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad Q^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&1\\-2&2&-1\\-1&1&-1\end{pmatrix}, \]

and

\[ D=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&\frac12&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Thus

\[ \boxed{A=QDQ^{-1}}. \]

Example 8 · Page 47

Given

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}-2&2&0\\1&1&-2\\0&2&-1\end{pmatrix}, \]

(a) show that the characteristic equation for \(M\) is

\[ \lambda^3+2\lambda^2+\lambda+4=0; \]

(b) verify that \(M\) satisfies its own characteristic equation;

(c) use the result to find \(M^{-1}\).

Show solution

(a) The characteristic equation is \(\det(M-\lambda I)=0\):

\[ \begin{vmatrix}-2-\lambda&2&0\\1&1-\lambda&-2\\0&2&-1-\lambda\end{vmatrix}=0. \]

Expanding,

\[ (-2-\lambda)\bigl((1-\lambda)(-1-\lambda)-(-2)(2)\bigr)-2\bigl(1(-1-\lambda)-0\bigr)=0, \]

which simplifies to

\[ \boxed{\lambda^3+2\lambda^2+\lambda+4=0}. \]

(b) Directly computing

\[ M^2=\begin{pmatrix}6&-2&-4\\-1&-1&0\\2&0&-3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad M^3=\begin{pmatrix}-14&2&8\\1&-3&2\\4&-2&3\end{pmatrix}, \]

gives

\[ M^3+2M^2+M+4I =\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

So \(M\) satisfies its own characteristic equation.

(c) From

\[ M^3+2M^2+M+4I=0, \]

multiplying through by \(M^{-1}\) gives

\[ M^2+2M+I+4M^{-1}=0, \]

so

\[ \boxed{M^{-1}=-\frac14\left(M^2+2M+I\right)}. \]

2020 May/June · 9231/21 & 9231/22 · Question 8 · 14 marks

(a) Find the values of \(a\) for which the system of equations

\[ 3x+y+z=0,\qquad ax+6y-z=0,\qquad ay-2z=0 \]

does not have a unique solution. [3]

The matrix \(A\) is given by

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1&1\\0&6&-1\\0&0&-2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Use the characteristic equation of \(A\) to find the inverse of \(A^2\). [4]

(c) Find a matrix \(P\) and a diagonal matrix \(D\) such that \(A^5=PDP^{-1}\). [7]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The system fails to have a unique solution exactly when \(\det M=0\):

\[ \begin{vmatrix}3&1&1\\a&6&-1\\0&a&-2\end{vmatrix} =3(-12+a)-(-2a)+a^2 =a^2+5a-36=0. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{a=4\ \text{or}\ a=-9}. \]

(b) The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(3\), \(6\), \(-2\), so the characteristic equation is

\[ (\lambda-3)(\lambda-6)(\lambda+2)=\lambda^3-7\lambda^2+36=0. \]

Thus \(A^3-7A^2+36I=0\), and multiplying through by \((A^{-1})^2\) gives

\[ 36(A^{-1})^2=7I-A. \]

Computing \((A^{-1})^2\) from this,

\[ \boxed{(A^{-1})^2=\frac1{36} \begin{pmatrix}4&-1&-1\\0&1&1\\0&0&9\end{pmatrix}}. \]

(c) With eigenvalues \(3,6,-2\) and corresponding eigenvectors

\[ \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}1\\3\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}-9\\5\\40\end{pmatrix}, \]

taking them as columns,

\[ P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&-9\\0&3&5\\0&0&40\end{pmatrix}, \qquad D=\begin{pmatrix}3^5&0&0\\0&6^5&0\\0&0&(-2)^5\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}243&0&0\\0&7776&0\\0&0&-32\end{pmatrix}. \]

Thus \(\boxed{A^5=PDP^{-1}}\).

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/M/J/2020, Q8; 9231/22/M/J/2020, Q8.

2020 May/June · 9231/23 · Question 3 · 8 marks

The matrix \(A\) is given by

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}5&-1&7\\0&6&0\\7&7&5\end{pmatrix}. \]

(a) Find the eigenvalues of \(A\). [4]

(b) Use the characteristic equation of \(A\) to find \(A^{-1}\). [4]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The characteristic equation is

\[ \begin{vmatrix}5-\lambda&-1&7\\0&6-\lambda&0\\7&7&5-\lambda\end{vmatrix}=0, \]

which expands to

\[ -\lambda^3+16\lambda^2-36\lambda-144=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (\lambda+2)(\lambda-6)(\lambda-12)=0. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{\lambda=-2,\ 6,\ 12}. \]

(b) The characteristic equation gives

\[ -A^3+16A^2-36A-144I=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; 144A^{-1}=-A^2+16A-36I. \]

Computing

\[ A^2=\begin{pmatrix}74&38&70\\0&36&0\\70&70&74\end{pmatrix}, \]

gives

\[ \boxed{A^{-1}=\frac1{24} \begin{pmatrix}-5&-9&7\\0&4&0\\7&7&-5\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/23/M/J/2020, Q3.

2020 October/November · 9231/21 & 9231/23 · Question 7 · 9 marks

The matrix \(P\) is given by

\[ P=\begin{pmatrix}1&4&2\\0&-1&1\\0&0&2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(a) State the eigenvalues of \(P\). [1]

(b) Use the characteristic equation of \(P\) to find \(P^{-1}\). [4]

The \(3\times3\) matrix \(A\) has distinct eigenvalues \(b\), \(-1\), \(1\) with corresponding eigenvectors

\[ \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(c) Find \(A\) in terms of \(b\). [4]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) For an upper triangular matrix the eigenvalues are the diagonal entries:

\[ \boxed{1,\ -1,\ 2}. \]

(b) The characteristic equation is

\[ (\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)(\lambda-2)=\lambda^3-2\lambda^2-\lambda+2=0, \]

so \(P^3-2P^2-P+2I=0\). Multiplying through by \(P^{-1}\),

\[ 2P^{-1}=I+2P-P^2. \]

Computing

\[ P^2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&10\\0&1&1\\0&0&4\end{pmatrix}, \]

gives

\[ \boxed{P^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&4&-3\\0&-1&\frac12\\0&0&\frac12\end{pmatrix}}. \]

(c) Using \(A=PDP^{-1}\) with

\[ D=\begin{pmatrix}b&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad P=\begin{pmatrix}1&4&2\\0&-1&1\\0&0&2\end{pmatrix}, \]

the eigenvectors are the columns of \(P\) in the same order as the eigenvalues in \(D\). Hence

\[ \boxed{A=\begin{pmatrix}b&4b+4&-3b-1\\0&-1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/21/O/N/2020, Q7; 9231/23/O/N/2020, Q7.

2020 October/November · 9231/22 · Question 9 · 15 marks

It is given that \(a\) is a positive constant.

(a) Show that the system of equations

\[ ax+(2a+5)y+(a+1)z=1,\qquad -4y=2,\qquad 3y-z=3 \]

has a unique solution, and interpret this situation geometrically. [3]

The matrix \(A\) is given by

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}a&2a+5&a+1\\0&-4&0\\0&3&-1\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Show that the eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(a\), \(-1\) and \(-4\). [2]

(c) Find a matrix \(P\) such that

\[ A=P\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&-4\end{pmatrix}P^{-1}. \]

[5]

(d) Use the characteristic equation of \(A\) to find \(A^{-1}\). [6]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

\[ \begin{vmatrix}a&2a+5&a+1\\0&-4&0\\0&3&-1\end{vmatrix}=4a\ne0 \]

since \(a>0\). Therefore the system has a unique solution: the three planes intersect at a single point.

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-2\\-1&1&2\\2&-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

(d) Use the characteristic equation of \(A\) to show that

\[ A^4=pA^2+qA \]

where \(p\) and \(q\) are integers to be determined. [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

\[ \begin{vmatrix}1&-1&2k\\k&1&2\\2&-1&1\end{vmatrix}=2k^2+3k+1=0. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{k=-1\ \text{or}\ k=-\frac12}. \]

(b) When \(k=-\frac12\), the equations become

\[ x-y-z=1,\qquad -\frac12x+y+2z=2,\qquad 2x-y+z=3. \]

Adding the first two equations gives \(\frac12x+z=3\), while the third equation gives \(2x-y+z=3\); eliminating variables leads to the contradiction \(1=-2\). Therefore the system is inconsistent. Geometrically, the planes form a triangular prism (no common point).

(c) When \(k=-1\), the equations become

\[ x-y-2z=1,\qquad -x+y+2z=2,\qquad 2x-y+z=3. \]

The first two equations give \(1=-2\), a contradiction. So the system is inconsistent. Geometrically, two planes are parallel (and not identical), with the third plane not parallel to them.

(d) The characteristic equation of \(A\) is

\[ \begin{vmatrix}1-\lambda&-1&-2\\-1&1-\lambda&2\\2&-1&1-\lambda\end{vmatrix} =-\lambda^3+3\lambda^2-8\lambda=0. \]

So \(A^3=3A^2-8A\). Multiplying by \(A\),

\[ A^4=3A^3-8A^2=3(3A^2-8A)-8A^2=A^2-24A. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{p=1,\ q=-24}. \]
