1.1 Roots of polynomial equations

根与系数 · 对称函数 · 建立新方程

CIE 9231 Paper 1，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Further Pure Mathematics 1 Chapter 3。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 记忆并使用二次、三次及四次多项式方程的根与系数关系；
  2. 计算根的对称函数，或利用这些关系求方程中的未知系数；
  3. 通过代换建立新方程，新方程的根与原方程的根具有简单关系。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Quadratic equations

若 \(az^2+bz+c=0\) 的根为 \(\alpha,\beta\)，则

\[ \alpha+\beta=-\frac ba, \qquad \alpha\beta=\frac ca. \]

二次方程中所有关于 \(\alpha,\beta\) 的对称函数，都可以用 \(\alpha+\beta\) 与 \(\alpha\beta\) 表示。例如

\[ \alpha^2+\beta^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta. \]

1.2 Cubic equations

若 \(az^3+bz^2+cz+d=0\) 的根为 \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\)，则

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum\alpha &=\alpha+\beta+\gamma=-\frac ba,\\ \sum\alpha\beta &=\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha=\frac ca,\\ \sum\alpha\beta\gamma &=\alpha\beta\gamma=-\frac da. \end{aligned} \]

教材采用以下简写：

\[ \sum\alpha=\alpha+\beta+\gamma, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta=\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha. \]

1.3 Common symmetric identities

对称函数的值不因根的排列次序改变。计算时通常先把所求式化为 \(\sum\alpha\)、\(\sum\alpha\beta\)、\(\alpha\beta\gamma\) 等基本对称函数。

对二次或三次方程的根，有

\[ \sum\alpha^2 =\left(\sum\alpha\right)^2-2\sum\alpha\beta. \]

对三次方程的根，有

\[ \sum\alpha^3 =\left(\sum\alpha\right)^3 -3\left(\sum\alpha\right)\left(\sum\alpha\beta\right) +3\alpha\beta\gamma, \]

以及

\[ \sum\alpha^2\beta^2 =\left(\sum\alpha\beta\right)^2 -2\left(\sum\alpha\right)\alpha\beta\gamma, \]

其中

\[ \sum\alpha^2\beta^2 =\alpha^2\beta^2+\beta^2\gamma^2+\gamma^2\alpha^2. \]

展开或因式分解时，应让最终表达式只含原方程系数能够确定的基本对称函数。

1.4 Power sums

设三次方程的根为 \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\)，并定义

\[ S_n=\alpha^n+\beta^n+\gamma^n. \]

若先把方程化为首一形式

\[ z^3+pz^2+qz+r=0, \]

则

\[ S_0=3, \qquad S_1=-p, \qquad S_2=p^2-2q. \]

因为每个根都满足原方程，将根的三个等式相加可得

\[ S_n+pS_{n-1}+qS_{n-2}+rS_{n-3}=0 \qquad(n\geqslant3). \]

特别地，当 \(n=3\) 时，常数项会出现三次：

\[ S_3+pS_2+qS_1+3r=0. \]

同理，若四次方程写成

\[ z^4+pz^3+qz^2+rz+s=0 \]

且 \(S_n\) 表示四个根的 \(n\) 次幂之和，则

\[ S_0=4 \]

并且

\[ S_n+pS_{n-1}+qS_{n-2}+rS_{n-3}+sS_{n-4}=0 \qquad(n\geqslant4). \]

这种方法适合连续计算较高次幂和；开始递推前，应先由根与系数关系求出所需的较低次幂和。

1.5 Reciprocal roots and negative powers

若原方程的常数项不为零，则所有根都不为零，可以讨论倒数根。 对三次方程的根，

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac1\alpha+\frac1\beta+\frac1\gamma &=\frac{\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma},\\ \frac1{\alpha\beta}+\frac1{\beta\gamma}+\frac1{\gamma\alpha} &=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}{\alpha\beta\gamma},\\ \frac1\alpha\frac1\beta\frac1\gamma &=\frac1{\alpha\beta\gamma}. \end{aligned} \]

因此，若 \(az^3+bz^2+cz+d=0\) 的根为 \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\)，其中 \(d\ne0\)，则

\[ \sum\frac1\alpha=-\frac cd, \qquad \sum\frac1{\alpha\beta}=\frac bd, \qquad \frac1{\alpha\beta\gamma}=-\frac ad. \]

求倒数根方程时，令 \(z=1/w\) 并乘以适当的 \(w\) 的幂。例如，

\[ az^3+bz^2+cz+d=0 \]

对应的倒数根方程为

\[ dw^3+cw^2+bw+a=0. \]

也就是说，系数的次序反转。相同方法适用于二次及四次方程。

负次幂和可以看作倒数根的正次幂和。例如

\[ \sum\alpha^{-n} =\left(\frac1\alpha\right)^n +\left(\frac1\beta\right)^n +\left(\frac1\gamma\right)^n, \]

所以可以先建立倒数根方程，再使用上一节的幂和方法。

1.6 Forming new equations

教材使用两种方法建立新方程：

  1. 计算新根的和、两两乘积之和及乘积，再由根与系数关系写出方程；
  2. 令新变量与旧变量满足题目给出的根的变换，写出旧变量并代入原方程。

例如新根为 \(2\alpha+1,2\beta+1,2\gamma+1\) 时，令

\[ w=2z+1, \qquad z=\frac{w-1}{2}. \]

新根为 \(\alpha^2,\beta^2,\gamma^2\) 时，可以令 \(w=z^2\)，再消去 \(z\)。

1.7 Quartic equations

若 \(az^4+bz^3+cz^2+dz+e=0\) 的根为 \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\)，则

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum\alpha &=\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta=-\frac ba,\\ \sum\alpha\beta &=\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta +\beta\gamma+\beta\delta+\gamma\delta=\frac ca,\\ \sum\alpha\beta\gamma &=\alpha\beta\gamma+\beta\gamma\delta +\gamma\delta\alpha+\delta\alpha\beta=-\frac da,\\ \sum\alpha\beta\gamma\delta &=\alpha\beta\gamma\delta=\frac ea. \end{aligned} \]

提示Sign pattern

从最高次项开始，根的基本对称函数对应的符号依次为

\[ -,\quad +,\quad -,\quad +. \]

2. Textbook examples and examination questions

Textbook Examples

Example 3.1 · Page 72

Find a quadratic equation with roots \(5\) and \(-3\).

Show solution

The sum of the roots is

\[ 5+(-3)=2, \]

and the product of the roots is

\[ 5(-3)=-15. \]

Taking the coefficient of \(z^2\) to be \(1\), a quadratic equation with the required roots is

\[ \boxed{z^2-2z-15=0}. \]

Example 3.2 · Page 73

The roots of the equation

\[ 2z^2+3z+5=0 \]

are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

  1. Find the values of \(\alpha+\beta\) and \(\alpha\beta\).
  2. Find the quadratic equation with roots \(2\alpha\) and \(2\beta\).
Show solution

From the original equation,

\[ \alpha+\beta=-\frac32, \qquad \alpha\beta=\frac52. \]

For the new roots,

\[ 2\alpha+2\beta=2(\alpha+\beta)=-3, \]

and

\[ (2\alpha)(2\beta)=4\alpha\beta=10. \]

Therefore the required equation is

\[ \boxed{z^2+3z+10=0}. \]

Example 3.3 · Pages 73–74

The roots of the equation

\[ 3z^2-4z-1=0 \]

are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Find the quadratic equation with roots \(\alpha+1\) and \(\beta+1\).

Show solution

From the original equation,

\[ \alpha+\beta=\frac43, \qquad \alpha\beta=-\frac13. \]

The sum of the new roots is

\[ (\alpha+1)+(\beta+1)=\alpha+\beta+2=\frac{10}{3}, \]

and their product is

\[ (\alpha+1)(\beta+1) =\alpha\beta+(\alpha+\beta)+1 =2. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{3z^2-10z+6=0}. \]

Example 3.4 · Pages 74–75

The quadratic equation

\[ ax^2+bx+c=0, \]

where \(a,b,c\) are constants, has roots \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Prove that

\[ \alpha^2+\beta^2=\frac{b^2-2ac}{a^2}. \]

Show solution

Since

\[ (\alpha+\beta)^2=\alpha^2+2\alpha\beta+\beta^2, \]

we have

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^2+\beta^2 &=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta\\ &=\left(-\frac ba\right)^2-2\left(\frac ca\right)\\ &=\boxed{\frac{b^2-2ac}{a^2}}. \end{aligned} \]

Example 3.5 · Page 78

The roots of the equation

\[ 2z^3-9z^2-27z+54=0 \]

form a geometric progression, so they may be written as \(\dfrac ar,a,ar\). Solve the equation.

Show solution

Using the product of the roots,

\[ \frac ar\times a\times ar=-\frac{54}{2}, \]

so

\[ a^3=-27 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=-3. \]

Using the sum of the roots,

\[ -\frac3r-3-3r=\frac92. \]

Hence

\[ 2r^2+5r+2=0, \]

so

\[ (2r+1)(r+2)=0 \]

and therefore \(r=-2\) or \(r=-\dfrac12\). Either value gives the same three roots:

\[ \boxed{\frac32,-3,6}. \]

Example 3.6 · Pages 78–79

The roots of the cubic equation

\[ x^3-4x^2+x+6=0 \]

are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Find the values of

  1. \(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2\);
  2. \(\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3\).
Show solution

From the original equation,

\[ \sum\alpha=4, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta=1. \]

Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2 &=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^2 -2(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha)\\ &=4^2-2(1)\\ &=\boxed{14}. \end{aligned} \]

Since each root satisfies \(x^3-4x^2+x+6=0\),

\[ \alpha^3=4\alpha^2-\alpha-6, \]

with corresponding equations for \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\). Adding them gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3 &=4(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2) -(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)-18\\ &=4(14)-4-18\\ &=\boxed{34}. \end{aligned} \]

Example 3.7 · Pages 79–80

The roots of the cubic equation

\[ 2z^3+5z^2-3z-2=0 \]

are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Find the cubic equation with roots \(2\alpha+1,2\beta+1,2\gamma+1\).

Show solution 1: relations between roots and coefficients

For the original equation,

\[ \sum\alpha=-\frac52, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta=-\frac32, \qquad \alpha\beta\gamma=1. \]

For the new roots, their sum is

\[ 2(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)+3=-2. \]

The sum of their products in pairs is

\[ \begin{aligned} &(2\alpha+1)(2\beta+1) +(2\beta+1)(2\gamma+1) +(2\gamma+1)(2\alpha+1)\\ &=4(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha) +4(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)+3\\ &=4\left(-\frac32\right)+4\left(-\frac52\right)+3\\ &=-13. \end{aligned} \]

Their product is

\[ \begin{aligned} &(2\alpha+1)(2\beta+1)(2\gamma+1)\\ &=8\alpha\beta\gamma +4(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha) +2(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)+1\\ &=8+4\left(-\frac32\right) +2\left(-\frac52\right)+1\\ &=-2. \end{aligned} \]

Hence the required equation is

\[ \boxed{z^3+2z^2-13z+2=0}. \]

Show solution 2: substitution method

Let

\[ w=2z+1, \qquad z=\frac{w-1}{2}. \]

Substituting into the original equation gives

\[ 2\left(\frac{w-1}{2}\right)^3 +5\left(\frac{w-1}{2}\right)^2 -3\left(\frac{w-1}{2}\right)-2=0. \]

Multiplying by \(4\) and simplifying,

\[ (w-1)^3+5(w-1)^2-6(w-1)-8=0, \]

so

\[ \boxed{w^3+2w^2-13w+2=0}. \]

Example 3.8 · Pages 80–81

The roots of the cubic equation

\[ 2z^3-5z^2+z+2=0 \]

are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Find the cubic equation with roots \(\alpha^2,\beta^2,\gamma^2\).

Show solution

Let \(w=z^2\), so \(z=\sqrt w\). Substitution gives

\[ 2w\sqrt w-5w+\sqrt w+2=0. \]

Collecting the terms containing \(\sqrt w\),

\[ \sqrt w(2w+1)=5w-2. \]

Squaring both sides,

\[ w(2w+1)^2=(5w-2)^2. \]

Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} 4w^3+4w^2+w&=25w^2-20w+4,\\ 4w^3-21w^2+21w-4&=0. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore the required equation is

\[ \boxed{4w^3-21w^2+21w-4=0}. \]

Example 3.9 · Pages 84–85

The roots of the quartic equation

\[ 4z^4+pz^3+qz^2-z+3=0 \]

are \(\alpha,-\alpha,\alpha+\lambda,\alpha-\lambda\), where \(\alpha\) and \(\lambda\) are real numbers.

  1. Express \(p\) and \(q\) in terms of \(\alpha\) and \(\lambda\).
  2. Show that \(\alpha=-\dfrac12\), and find the values of \(p\) and \(q\).
  3. Give the roots of the quartic equation.
Show solution

The sum of the roots is

\[ \alpha-\alpha+(\alpha+\lambda)+(\alpha-\lambda) =2\alpha=-\frac p4, \]

so

\[ \boxed{p=-8\alpha}. \]

The sum of the products of the roots in pairs simplifies to \(-\lambda^2\). Hence

\[ -\lambda^2=\frac q4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{q=-4\lambda^2}. \]

The sum of the products of the roots in threes is

\[ -2\alpha^3. \]

Since this is equal to \(-\dfrac{-1}{4}=\dfrac14\),

\[ -2\alpha^3=\frac14. \]

Thus

\[ \alpha^3=-\frac18 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\alpha=-\frac12}, \]

and therefore

\[ \boxed{p=4}. \]

Using the product of the four roots,

\[ -\alpha^2(\alpha^2-\lambda^2)=\frac34. \]

Substituting \(\alpha=-\dfrac12\) gives

\[ -\frac14\left(\frac14-\lambda^2\right)=\frac34, \]

so

\[ \lambda^2=\frac{13}{4} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \boxed{q=-13}. \]

The roots are

\[ \boxed{ \frac12, -\frac12, -\frac12+\frac{\sqrt{13}}2, -\frac12-\frac{\sqrt{13}}2 }. \]

Exercise 3B, Question 16

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Q5 October/November 2015

The cubic equation

\[ x^3+px^2+qx+r=0, \]

where \(p,q,r\) are integers, has roots \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) such that

\[ \alpha+\beta+\gamma=15, \qquad \alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2=83. \]

Write down the value of \(p\) and find the value of \(q\).

Given that \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are all real and that \(\alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma=36\), find \(\alpha\) and hence find the value of \(r\).

Show solution

Since \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=-p\),

\[ \boxed{p=-15}. \]

Also,

\[ \begin{aligned} 83 &=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^2 -2(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha)\\ &=15^2-2q, \end{aligned} \]

so

\[ \boxed{q=71}. \]

Now

\[ \alpha\beta+\alpha\gamma =\alpha(\beta+\gamma) =\alpha(15-\alpha)=36. \]

Thus

\[ \alpha^2-15\alpha+36=0, \]

giving \(\alpha=3\) or \(\alpha=12\).

If \(\alpha=12\), then \(\beta+\gamma=3\) and \(\beta\gamma=35\), which does not give real values of \(\beta,\gamma\). Hence

\[ \boxed{\alpha=3}. \]

Then \(\beta+\gamma=12\) and \(\beta\gamma=35\), so

\[ r=-\alpha\beta\gamma=-3(35)=\boxed{-105}. \]

Exercise 3B, Question 17

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Q2 October/November 2013

The cubic equation

\[ x^3-px-q=0, \]

where \(p,q\) are constants, has roots \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Show that

  1. \(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2=2p\);
  2. \(\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3=3q\);
  3. \[ 6(\alpha^5+\beta^5+\gamma^5) =5(\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3) (\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2). \]
Show solution

For the original equation,

\[ \alpha+\beta+\gamma=0, \qquad \alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha=-p. \]

Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2 &=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^2 -2(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha)\\ &=0-2(-p)\\ &=\boxed{2p}. \end{aligned} \]

Each root satisfies \(x^3=px+q\). Adding the three corresponding equations gives

\[ \alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3 =p(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)+3q =\boxed{3q}. \]

Multiplying \(x^3=px+q\) by \(x^2\) gives \(x^5=px^3+qx^2\). Hence

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^5+\beta^5+\gamma^5 &=p(\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3) +q(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2)\\ &=p(3q)+q(2p)\\ &=5pq. \end{aligned} \]

Thus both sides of the required identity are equal to \(30pq\).

Exercise 3B, Question 18

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Q1 October/November 2011

The equation

\[ x^3+px+q=0 \]

has a repeated root. Prove that

\[ 4p^3+27q^2=0. \]

Show solution

Let the repeated root be \(\alpha\) and the third root be \(\beta\). Since the coefficient of \(x^2\) is zero,

\[ 2\alpha+\beta=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \beta=-2\alpha. \]

Using the sum of the products in pairs,

\[ p=\alpha^2+2\alpha\beta =\alpha^2-4\alpha^2 =-3\alpha^2. \]

Using the product of the roots,

\[ -q=\alpha^2\beta=-2\alpha^3, \]

so \(q=2\alpha^3\). Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} 4p^3+27q^2 &=4(-3\alpha^2)^3+27(2\alpha^3)^2\\ &=-108\alpha^6+108\alpha^6\\ &=\boxed{0}. \end{aligned} \]

Exercise 3C, Question 7

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Q11 October/November 2014

The roots of the quartic equation

\[ x^4+4x^3+2x^2-4x+1=0 \]

are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\). Find the values of

  1. \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta\);
  2. \(\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2+\delta^2\);
  3. \(\dfrac1\alpha+\dfrac1\beta+\dfrac1\gamma+\dfrac1\delta\);
  4. \[ \frac{\alpha}{\beta\gamma\delta} +\frac{\beta}{\alpha\gamma\delta} +\frac{\gamma}{\alpha\beta\delta} +\frac{\delta}{\alpha\beta\gamma}. \]

Using the substitution \(y=x+1\), find a quartic equation in \(y\). Solve this quartic equation and hence find the roots of

\[ x^4+4x^3+2x^2-4x+1=0. \]

Show solution

From the coefficients,

\[ \sum\alpha=-4, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta=2, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta\gamma=4, \qquad \alpha\beta\gamma\delta=1. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta=-4}, \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2+\delta^2 &=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta)^2-2\sum\alpha\beta\\ &=16-4\\ &=\boxed{12}, \end{aligned} \]

and

\[ \frac1\alpha+\frac1\beta+\frac1\gamma+\frac1\delta =\frac{\sum\alpha\beta\gamma}{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} =\boxed{4}. \]

Also,

\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{\alpha}{\beta\gamma\delta} +\frac{\beta}{\alpha\gamma\delta} +\frac{\gamma}{\alpha\beta\delta} +\frac{\delta}{\alpha\beta\gamma}\\ &=\frac{\alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2+\delta^2} {\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\\ &=\boxed{12}. \end{aligned} \]

Let \(y=x+1\), so \(x=y-1\). Substitution gives

\[ y^4-4y^2+4=0, \]

or

\[ (y^2-2)^2=0. \]

Thus \(y=\pm\sqrt2\), each repeated, and the roots of the original equation are

\[ \boxed{-1+\sqrt2,\ -1+\sqrt2,\ -1-\sqrt2,\ -1-\sqrt2}. \]

Exercise 3C, Question 8

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Q11 October/November 2012

The roots of the equation

\[ x^4-3x^2+5x-2=0 \]

are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\), and

\[ S_n=\alpha^n+\beta^n+\gamma^n+\delta^n. \]

Show that

\[ S_{n+4}-3S_{n+2}+5S_{n+1}-2S_n=0. \]

Find the values of

  1. \(S_2\) and \(S_4\);
  2. \(S_3\) and \(S_5\).

Hence find the value of

\[ \begin{aligned} &\alpha^2(\beta^3+\gamma^3+\delta^3) +\beta^2(\gamma^3+\delta^3+\alpha^3)\\ &\quad+\gamma^2(\delta^3+\alpha^3+\beta^3) +\delta^2(\alpha^3+\beta^3+\gamma^3). \end{aligned} \]

Show solution

Each root satisfies

\[ x^4-3x^2+5x-2=0. \]

Multiplying by \(x^n\) and then adding the four resulting equations gives

\[ \boxed{S_{n+4}-3S_{n+2}+5S_{n+1}-2S_n=0}. \]

From the coefficients,

\[ S_1=0, \qquad \sum\alpha\beta=-3, \]
