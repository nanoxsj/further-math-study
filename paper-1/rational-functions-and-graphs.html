1.2 Rational functions and graphs

有理函数 · 渐近线 · 值域 · 图像变换

CIE 9231 Paper 1，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Further Pure Mathematics 1 Chapter 4。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 作出分子、分母次数均不超过 2 的简单有理函数图像；
  2. 求水平、竖直及斜渐近线；
  3. 求函数的值域，包括使用判别式的方法；
  4. 在草图中表示转折点、渐近线以及与坐标轴的交点；
  5. 使用 \(y=f(x)\) 与 \(y=|f(x)|\)、\(y=f(|x|)\)、\(y=1/f(x)\)、\(y^2=f(x)\) 之间的关系；
  6. 使用图像解方程和不等式。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Rational functions

有理函数写成

\[ y=\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}, \]

其中 \(f(x)\) 与 \(g(x)\) 是多项式，且 \(g(x)\ne0\)。本章只讨论分子与分母次数不超过 2 的情形。

1.2 Intercepts and asymptotes

作图时依次确定以下信息。

  1. 与坐标轴的交点：令 \(x=0\) 求 \(y\) 轴交点；令分子等于零求 \(x\) 轴交点。
  2. 竖直渐近线：函数在 \(g(x)=0\) 处无定义时，检查该点两侧的符号与趋向。
  3. 水平渐近线：比较 \(x\to\pm\infty\) 时分子与分母的最高次项。
  4. 斜渐近线：当分子的次数比分母高 1 时，作多项式除法，将函数写成

\[ y=ax+b+\frac{r}{g(x)}. \]

当 \(x\to\pm\infty\) 时，余项趋于零，所以斜渐近线为 \(y=ax+b\)。

1.3 A five-step sketching method

教材使用以下五步作图法：

  1. 求图像与坐标轴的交点；
  2. 求竖直渐近线，并判断曲线在渐近线两侧趋向 \(+\infty\) 还是 \(-\infty\)；
  3. 求水平或斜渐近线，并判断曲线从哪一侧接近；
  4. 求驻点；
  5. 综合所有信息完成草图。

1.4 Range by the discriminant method

把有理函数方程重新整理为关于 \(x\) 的二次方程。对于给定的 \(y\)，图像上存在实点的条件是

\[ \Delta\ge0. \]

因此，令 \(\Delta<0\) 可以找出函数不能取得的 \(y\) 值；再结合渐近线是否能够取到端点，即可写出值域。

1.5 Symmetry

  • 若 \(f(x)=f(-x)\)，则 \(f\) 是偶函数，图像关于 \(y\) 轴对称。
  • 若 \(f(x)=-f(-x)\)，则 \(f\) 是奇函数，图像关于原点具有二阶旋转对称性。

1.6 The curve \(y=|f(x)|\)

先画 \(y=f(x)\)，再把其中位于 \(x\) 轴下方的部分关于 \(x\) 轴反射。原图上的根保持不变，但通常在根处形成尖点。

1.7 The curve \(y=f(|x|)\)

保留 \(y=f(x)\) 在 \(x\ge0\) 的部分，再把这一部分关于 \(y\) 轴反射。所得图像一定关于 \(y\) 轴对称。

1.8 The reciprocal curve \(y=1/f(x)\)

由 \(y=f(x)\) 作 \(y=1/f(x)\) 时：

  • 两图在 \(y=1\) 与 \(y=-1\) 处相交；
  • 两图在同一 \(x\) 处符号相同；
  • \(f(x)=0\) 的根通常变成 \(1/f(x)\) 的竖直渐近线；
  • \(f(x)\) 的竖直渐近线变成 \(1/f(x)\) 在 \(x\) 轴上的间断点；
  • 若 \(f(x)\to L\ne0\)，则 \(1/f(x)\to1/L\)；
  • \(f\) 增加时 \(1/f\) 减少，\(f\) 减少时 \(1/f\) 增加。

1.9 The curve \(y^2=f(x)\)

方程等价于

\[ y=\pm\sqrt{f(x)}. \]

因此：

  • 图像关于 \(x\) 轴对称；
  • 当 \(f(x)<0\) 时没有实点；
  • 与 \(y=f(x)\) 在 \(y=0\) 和 \(y=1\) 处相交；
  • 若 \(f(x)\) 的根不是驻点，\(y^2=f(x)\) 通常竖直穿过 \(x\) 轴；
  • 对应驻点的 \(x\) 坐标不变，而 \(y\) 坐标变为 \(\pm\sqrt{f(x)}\)。

2. Textbook examples and examination questions

Textbook Examples

Example 4.1 · Pages 96–97

Let

\[ f(x)=\frac{x^2+1}{x^2+2}. \]

(i) Sketch the graph of \(y=f(x)\).

(ii) State the range of \(f(x)\).

(iii) The equation \(f(x)=k\) has no real solutions. Find the values of \(k\).

Show solution

There are no real zeros of either \(x^2+1\) or \(x^2+2\). Hence the curve has no \(x\)-intercepts and no vertical asymptotes. It passes through

\[ \left(0,\frac12\right). \]

As \(x\to\pm\infty\),

\[ \frac{x^2+1}{x^2+2}\to1, \]

so \(y=1\) is a horizontal asymptote, approached from below. Also

\[ f'(x)=\frac{2x}{(x^2+2)^2}, \]

so the only stationary point is the minimum \(\left(0,\frac12\right)\). The graph is symmetrical about the \(y\)-axis.

Therefore

\[ \boxed{\frac12\le f(x)<1}. \]

The equation \(f(x)=k\) has no real solutions when

\[ \boxed{k<\frac12\quad\text{or}\quad k\ge1}. \]

Example 4.2 · Pages 103–104

The graph of \(y=f(x)\) has stationary points

\[ P(-1,-10),\qquad Q(3,5). \]

Sketch:

  1. \(y=|f(x)|\);
  2. \(y=f(|x|)\).
Show solution

For \(y=|f(x)|\), retain every part of the original curve above the \(x\)-axis and reflect every part below the \(x\)-axis in the \(x\)-axis. In particular, \(P(-1,-10)\) is mapped to \((-1,10)\), while \(Q(3,5)\) is unchanged.

For \(y=f(|x|)\), retain only the original curve for \(x\ge0\) and reflect this part in the \(y\)-axis. Thus \(Q(3,5)\) is accompanied by the reflected stationary point \((-3,5)\); the original left-hand part, including \(P\), is not used.

Example 4.3 · Pages 105–106

Given

\[ f(x)=\cos x, \qquad g(x)=\tan x, \]

sketch

\[ y=\frac1{f(x)} \qquad\text{and}\qquad y=\frac1{g(x)} \]

for \(-360^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\), where \(g(x)\) is undefined at \(x=\pm90^\circ,\pm270^\circ\).

Show solution

The first curve is

\[ y=\sec x. \]

It has vertical asymptotes where \(\cos x=0\), namely

\[ x=\pm90^\circ,\ \pm270^\circ, \]

and vertices at the points where \(\cos x=\pm1\).

The second curve is

\[ y=\cot x. \]

It has vertical asymptotes where \(\tan x=0\), at \(x=-360^\circ,-180^\circ,0,180^\circ,360^\circ\). It has zeros at \(x=\pm90^\circ,\pm270^\circ\), shown as open points here because the stated function \(g\) is itself undefined at those values.

Example 4.4 · Page 107

The curve \(y=f(x)\) has asymptotes \(x=1\) and \(y=2\), and intercepts \((0,4)\) and \((2,0)\). Sketch

\[ y=\frac1{f(x)}. \]

Show solution
  • The root \(x=2\) becomes the vertical asymptote \(x=2\).
  • The horizontal asymptote \(y=2\) becomes \(y=\frac12\).
  • The vertical asymptote \(x=1\) becomes an open point on the \(x\)-axis because \(1/f(1)\) is undefined while \(1/f(x)\to0\) as \(x\to1\).
  • The point \((0,4)\) maps to \(\left(0,\frac14\right)\).
  • The reciprocal graph has the same sign as the original graph and reverses its increasing/decreasing behaviour.

Example 4.5 · Pages 108–109

(i) Given \(f(x)=(x-3)^2\), sketch \(y=f(x)\) and \(y^2=f(x)\) on the same axes.

(ii) Given \(f(x)=\cos x\) for \(-2\pi\le x\le2\pi\), sketch \(y=f(x)\) and \(y^2=f(x)\) on the same axes.

Show solution

(i)

\[ y^2=(x-3)^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=\pm(x-3). \]

Thus the transformed graph is the pair of straight lines \(y=x-3\) and \(y=3-x\). It meets the parabola at \((3,0)\), \((2,1)\) and \((4,1)\).

(ii)

\[ y=\pm\sqrt{\cos x}. \]

Real points occur only where \(\cos x\ge0\). Draw the upper curve \(y=\sqrt{\cos x}\) on those intervals and reflect it in the \(x\)-axis. The transformed curves meet \(y=\cos x\) where \(y=0\) or \(y=1\).

Example 4.6 · Pages 110–111

The graph of \(y=f(x)\) has a maximum at \((-1,4)\), roots at \((-2,0)\), \((1,0)\) and \((3,0)\), and \(y\)-intercept \((0,3)\). Sketch \(y^2=f(x)\), showing all turning points and intersections with the axes.

Show solution

The transformed graph exists only where \(f(x)\ge0\) and is symmetrical about the \(x\)-axis. The maximum \((-1,4)\) produces two turning points

\[ (-1,2),\qquad(-1,-2). \]

The \(y\)-intercept produces

\[ (0,\sqrt3),\qquad(0,-\sqrt3). \]

The roots remain

\[ (-2,0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(3,0). \]

At each non-stationary root the transformed curve meets the \(x\)-axis vertically. There is no transformed curve on intervals where \(f(x)<0\).

Cambridge examination questions in the textbook

Exercise 4B, Question 4

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{2x^2+x-1}{x-1}. \]

Find the equations of the asymptotes of \(C\). Show that there is no point on \(C\) for which \(1<y<9\).

Show solution

Since

\[ \frac{2x^2+x-1}{x-1}=2x+3+\frac2{x-1}, \]

the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=1},\qquad\boxed{y=2x+3}. \]

Rearranging as a quadratic in \(x\) gives

\[ 2x^2+(1-y)x+(y-1)=0. \]

Real \(x\) requires

\[ (1-y)^2-8(y-1)\ge0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (y-1)(y-9)\ge0. \]

Thus \(y\le1\) or \(y\ge9\), so no point has \(1<y<9\).

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics 9231 Paper 11, Question 4, October/November 2014.

Exercise 4B, Question 5

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{x^2+px+1}{x-2}, \]

where \(p\) is a constant.

  1. Given that \(C\) has two asymptotes, find the equation of each asymptote.
  2. Find the set of values of \(p\) for which \(C\) has two distinct turning points.
  3. Sketch \(C\) in the case \(p=-1\), indicating the coordinates of any intersections with the axes.
Show solution

Polynomial division gives

\[ y=x+p+2+\frac{2p+5}{x-2}. \]

Hence the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=2},\qquad\boxed{y=x+p+2}. \]

Also

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=1-\frac{2p+5}{(x-2)^2}. \]

There are two distinct turning points precisely when \(2p+5>0\), so

\[ \boxed{p>-\frac52}. \]

For \(p=-1\),

\[ y=x+1+\frac3{x-2}. \]

The asymptotes are \(x=2\) and \(y=x+1\). The curve has no \(x\)-intercepts because \(x^2-x+1=0\) has negative discriminant, and its \(y\)-intercept is \(\left(0,-\frac12\right)\).

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics 9231 Paper 11, Question 7, October/November 2011.

Exercise 4B, Question 6

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{2x^2+kx}{x+1}, \]

where \(k\) is a constant.

  1. Find the set of values of \(k\) for which \(C\) has no stationary points.
  2. For \(k=4\), find the equations of the asymptotes and sketch \(C\), indicating its intersections with the coordinate axes.
Show solution

Differentiation gives a stationary-point equation whose discriminant is proportional to \(2-k\). Hence there are no stationary points when

\[ \boxed{k\ge2}. \]

For \(k=4\),

\[ y=2x+2-\frac2{x+1}. \]

Therefore the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=-1},\qquad\boxed{y=2x+2}, \]

and the intersections with the axes are

\[ (-2,0),\qquad(0,0). \]

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics 9231 Paper 11, Question 8, October/November 2015.

Exercise 4B, Question 7

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{px^2+4x+1}{x+1}, \]

where \(p\) is a positive constant and \(p\ne3\).

  1. Obtain the equations of the asymptotes of \(C\).
  2. Find the value of \(p\) for which the \(x\)-axis is tangent to \(C\), and sketch \(C\) in this case.
  3. For \(p=1\), show that \(C\) has no turning points, and sketch \(C\), giving the exact coordinates of its intersections with the \(x\)-axis.
Show solution

Division gives

\[ y=px+4-p+\frac{p-3}{x+1}, \]

so the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=-1},\qquad\boxed{y=px+4-p}. \]

Tangency to the \(x\)-axis requires \(px^2+4x+1=0\) to have a repeated root. Hence

\[ 16-4p=0, \]

so \(\boxed{p=4}\) and the tangent point is \(\left(-\frac12,0\right)\).

For \(p=1\),

\[ y=\frac{x^2+4x+1}{x+1}=x+3-\frac2{x+1}. \]

Then

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=1+\frac2{(x+1)^2}>0, \]

so there are no turning points. The \(x\)-intercepts are

\[ \boxed{\left(-2-\sqrt3,0\right),\quad\left(-2+\sqrt3,0\right)}. \]

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics 9231 Paper 11, Question 10, October/November 2013.

Exercise 4B, Question 8

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\lambda x+\frac{x}{x-2}, \]

where \(\lambda\) is a non-zero constant.

  1. Find the equations of the asymptotes of \(C\).
  2. Show that \(C\) has no turning points if \(\lambda<0\).
  3. Sketch \(C\) for \(\lambda=-1\), stating the coordinates of the intersections with the axes.
Show solution

Since

\[ y=\lambda x+1+\frac2{x-2}, \]

the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=2},\qquad\boxed{y=\lambda x+1}. \]

Also

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=\lambda-\frac2{(x-2)^2}. \]

If \(\lambda<0\), both terms are negative wherever the function is defined, so there are no turning points.

For \(\lambda=-1\),

\[ y=-x+1+\frac2{x-2}, \]

with asymptotes \(x=2\) and \(y=-x+1\). The intersections with the axes are

\[ \boxed{(0,0),\quad(3,0)}. \]

Source: Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics 9231 Paper 11, Question 7, October/November 2012.

Two curvesAsymptotesStationary points

The curves \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) have equations

\[ C_1:\ y=\frac{ax}{x+5}, \qquad C_2:\ y=\frac{x^2+(a+10)x+5a+26}{x+5}, \]

respectively, where \(a>2\).

(i) Find the equations of the asymptotes of \(C_1\). [2]

(ii) Find the equation of the oblique asymptote of \(C_2\). [2]

(iii) Show that \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) do not intersect. [2]

(iv) Find the coordinates of the stationary points of \(C_2\). [3]

(v) Sketch \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) on a single diagram. You do not need to calculate the coordinates of any points where \(C_2\) crosses the axes. [3]

Show answer and mark scheme

For \(C_1\), the asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=-5},\qquad\boxed{y=a}. \]

For \(C_2\),

\[ x^2+(a+10)x+5a+26=(x+5)(x+a+5)+1, \]

so its oblique asymptote is

\[ \boxed{y=x+a+5}. \]

Equating the two functions gives

\[ x^2+10x+5a+26=0. \]

Its discriminant is

\[ 100-4(5a+26)=-4-20a<0, \]

so the curves do not intersect.

Differentiating \(C_2\) gives the stationary-point equation

\[ x^2+10x+24=0, \]

hence the stationary points are

\[ \boxed{(-4,a+2),\quad(-6,a-2)}. \]

The sketch must show the common vertical asymptote \(x=-5\), the stated horizontal/oblique asymptotes, the two stationary points of \(C_2\), and no intersection between the curves.

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/11/M/J/2019, Q10; 9231/12/M/J/2019, Q10.

2019 May/June · 9231/13 · Question 6 · 9 marks

Oblique asymptoteStationary points

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{x^2}{kx-1}, \]

where \(k\) is a positive constant.

(i) Obtain the equations of the asymptotes of \(C\). [3]

(ii) Find the coordinates of the stationary points of \(C\). [3]

(iii) Sketch \(C\). [3]

Show answer and mark scheme

The asymptotes are

\[ \boxed{x=\frac1k}, \qquad \boxed{y=\frac{x}{k}+\frac1{k^2}}. \]

Also

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x(kx-2)}{(kx-1)^2}, \]

so the stationary points are

\[ \boxed{(0,0)}, \qquad \boxed{\left(\frac2k,\frac4{k^2}\right)}. \]

The branch containing the origin touches the \(x\)-axis at \((0,0)\); the other branch has its minimum at \(\left(2/k,4/k^2\right)\). Both branches approach the stated asymptotes.

Source: Cambridge International 9231/13/M/J/2019, Q6.

2019 October/November · 9231/11, 9231/12 & 9231/13 · Question 4 · 7 marks

Given asymptoteUnknown constants

The line \(y=2x+1\) is an asymptote of the curve \(C\) with equation

\[ y=\frac{x^2+1}{ax+b}. \]

(i) Find the values of \(a\) and \(b\). [3]

(ii) State the equation of the other asymptote of \(C\). [1]

(iii) Sketch \(C\), indicating the coordinates of any point of intersection with the \(y\)-axis. You do not need to find the stationary points. [3]

Show answer and mark scheme

Matching the quotient \(2x+1\) gives

\[ \boxed{a=\frac12},\qquad\boxed{b=-\frac14}. \]

The other asymptote is

\[ \boxed{x=\frac12}. \]

The \(y\)-intercept is

\[ \boxed{(0,-4)}. \]

The two branches approach \(x=\frac12\) and \(y=2x+1\) with the correct signs; the numerator has no real zero, so the curve does not cross the \(x\)-axis.

Let \(a\) be a positive constant.

(a) Sketch

\[ y=\frac{ax}{x+7}. \]

[2]

(b) Sketch

\[ y=\left|\frac{ax}{x+7}\right| \]

and find the set of values of \(x\) for which

\[ \left|\frac{ax}{x+7}\right|>\frac a2. \]

[4]

Show answer and mark scheme

The original curve has asymptotes \(x=-7\) and \(y=a\), and passes through \((0,0)\). Reflect its negative part in the \(x\)-axis to obtain the modulus graph.

The critical equations are

\[ \frac{ax}{x+7}=\pm\frac a2, \]

giving \(x=7\) and \(x=-\frac73\). Hence

\[ \boxed{x<-7\quad\text{or}\quad-7<x<-\frac73\quad\text{or}\quad x>7}. \]

2020 May/June · 9231/13 · Question 6 · 15 marks

No turning pointsModulusInequality

The curve \(C\) has equation

\[ y=\frac{10+x-2x^2}{2x-3}. \]

(a) Find the equations of the asymptotes of \(C\). [3]

(b) Show that \(C\) has no turning points. [3]

(c) Sketch \(C\), stating the coordinates of the intersections with the axes. [3]

(d) Sketch

\[ y=\left|\frac{10+x-2x^2}{2x-3}\right| \]

and find in exact form the set of values of \(x\) for which

\[ \left|\frac{10+x-2x^2}{2x-3}\right|<4. \]

[6]

Differentiation gives the numerator \(-(x^2+4x+5)\), whose discriminant is negative, so there are no stationary points. The intersections are

\[ \boxed{(-2,0),\qquad(0,2)}. \]

Solving the boundary equations \(f(x)=\pm2\) and reading the intervals from the modulus sketch gives

\[ \boxed{ -\frac74-\frac{\sqrt{17}}4<x<-\frac52 \quad\text{or}\quad -\frac74+\frac{\sqrt{17}}4<x<0, \qquad x\ne\frac{-3\pm\sqrt5}{2}}. \]
