1.7 Proof by induction

Base case · Inductive hypothesis · Inductive step · Conclusion

CIE 9231 Paper 1，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Further Pure Mathematics 1 Sections 2.4–2.5。

1. Syllabus knowledge

重要Syllabus objective

Use the method of mathematical induction to establish a given result.

题目可以涉及有限和、递推数列、不等式、导数或矩阵幂；证明结构相同，但 inductive step 的运算对象不同。

1.1 The logical chain

设命题为 \(P(n)\)，起始整数为 \(n=n_0\)。完整证明必须包含：

  1. Base case: 验证 \(P(n_0)\)；
  2. Inductive hypothesis: 假设 \(P(k)\) 对某个 \(k\ge n_0\) 成立；
  3. Target: 明确 \(P(k+1)\) 应是什么；
  4. Inductive step: 只使用 \(P(k)\) 与已知事实推出 \(P(k+1)\)；
  5. Conclusion: 写出由 mathematical induction，\(P(n)\) 对所有 \(n\ge n_0\) 成立。

\[ P(n_0),\; P(k)\Longrightarrow P(k+1) \;\Longrightarrow\; P(n)\text{ for every integer }n\ge n_0. \]

1.2 A mark-scheme-ready template

提示Proof template

Base case. When \(n=n_0\), show that LHS = RHS (or that the stated inequality is true).

Hypothesis. Assume the result is true for \(n=k\), where \(k\ge n_0\):

\[ P(k). \]

Step. For \(n=k+1\), begin from the side that contains the \(k\)-case, apply the hypothesis, and simplify until the target \(P(k+1)\) is obtained.

Conclusion. Therefore \(P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)\). Since \(P(n_0)\) is true, the result follows by mathematical induction for all integers \(n\ge n_0\).

1.3 Sums of series

If

\[ S_n=\sum_{r=1}^{n}f(r)=F(n), \]

then the \((k+1)\) case begins with

\[ S_{k+1}=S_k+f(k+1). \]

Use the hypothesis \(S_k=F(k)\), then show

\[ F(k)+f(k+1)=F(k+1). \]

Do not assume the target \(F(k+1)\) at the beginning of the inductive step.

1.4 Recursively defined sequences

For \(u_{n+1}=g(u_n)\) and a proposed formula \(u_n=F(n)\), assume \(u_k=F(k)\) and write

\[ u_{k+1}=g(u_k)=g(F(k)). \]

The target is \(F(k+1)\). The recurrence, not the proposed answer, must be the starting point.

1.5 Inequalities

For an inequality, the hypothesis supplies a bound rather than an equality. Every multiplication or division must respect signs. A typical step is

\[ A_{k+1}=c_kA_k>c_kB_k\ge B_{k+1}, \]

where the proof must explain why \(c_k>0\) and why the second comparison holds.

The base case must use the first value for which the statement is claimed, which need not be \(1\).

1.6 Derivatives

If a formula is proposed for \(D^n[f(x)]\), then

\[ D^{k+1}[f(x)]=\frac{d}{dx}\left(D^k[f(x)]\right). \]

Differentiate the expression supplied by the hypothesis and simplify to the target with \(k\) replaced by \(k+1\).

1.7 Matrix powers

For a proposed expression \(A^n=M_n\), use

\[ A^{k+1}=A^kA=M_kA. \]

Carry out the matrix multiplication explicitly and transform every entry into the corresponding entry of \(M_{k+1}\). Do not treat matrix multiplication as elementwise multiplication.

1.8 Why all parts are necessary

  • Checking several numerical cases gives evidence, not proof.
  • Proving only \(P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)\) is insufficient without a valid base case.
  • A correct base case alone does not establish later cases.
  • The conclusion must state the domain, such as all positive integers or all integers \(n\ge4\).

2. Textbook Examples

本节保留教材 Sections 2.4–2.5 的全部编号 Example 2.9–2.13。

Prove, for every positive integer \(n\), that

\[ 1^2+2^2+\cdots+n^2=\frac16n(n+1)(2n+1). \]

提示Solution

For \(n=1\),

\[ \text{LHS}=1, \; \text{RHS}=\frac16(1)(2)(3)=1. \]

Assume the result is true for \(n=k\):

\[ 1^2+2^2+\cdots+k^2=\frac16k(k+1)(2k+1). \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} 1^2+\cdots+k^2+(k+1)^2 &=\frac16k(k+1)(2k+1)+(k+1)^2\\ &=\frac16(k+1)\{k(2k+1)+6(k+1)\}\\ &=\frac16(k+1)(2k^2+7k+6)\\ &=\frac16(k+1)(k+2)(2k+3). \end{aligned} \]

This is the required formula with \(n=k+1\). Therefore the implication from \(k\) to \(k+1\) holds. Since the result is true for \(n=1\), it is true for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

The sequence is defined by

\[ u_{n+1}=4u_n-3, \; u_1=2. \]

Prove that \(u_n=4^{n-1}+1\).

提示Solution

For \(n=1\), the formula gives \(u_1=4^0+1=2\), so the base case is true.

Assume that for \(n=k\),

\[ u_k=4^{k-1}+1. \]

Using the recurrence,

\[ \begin{aligned} u_{k+1}&=4u_k-3\\ &=4(4^{k-1}+1)-3\\ &=4^k+1. \end{aligned} \]

This is the proposed formula for \(n=k+1\). Hence the result is true for all positive integers \(n\) by mathematical induction.

Prove that \(n!>2^n\) for every integer \(n\ge4\).

提示Solution

For \(n=4\),

\[ 4!=24>16=2^4. \]

Assume that \(k!>2^k\) for some integer \(k\ge4\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} (k+1)!&=(k+1)k!\\ &>(k+1)2^k\\ &>2\cdot2^k\\ &=2^{k+1}, \end{aligned} \]

because \(k+1>2\). Thus the result for \(k\) implies the result for \(k+1\). Since it is true for \(n=4\), it follows by mathematical induction that \(n!>2^n\) for every integer \(n\ge4\).

Prove that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^n}{dx^n}(xe^{ax})=na^{n-1}e^{ax}+a^nxe^{ax}, \]

where \(a\) is constant.

提示Solution

For \(n=1\), the product rule gives

\[ \frac d{dx}(xe^{ax})=e^{ax}+axe^{ax}, \]

which agrees with the formula.

Assume that

\[ \frac{d^k}{dx^k}(xe^{ax})=ka^{k-1}e^{ax}+a^kxe^{ax}. \]

Differentiate once more:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}}{dx^{k+1}}(xe^{ax}) &=\frac d{dx}\left(ka^{k-1}e^{ax}+a^kxe^{ax}\right)\\ &=ka^ke^{ax}+a^ke^{ax}+a^{k+1}xe^{ax}\\ &=(k+1)a^ke^{ax}+a^{k+1}xe^{ax}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the required expression for \(n=k+1\). The formula therefore holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

Given

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\3&2\end{pmatrix}, \]

prove that

\[ A^n=\frac14 \begin{pmatrix} 3\cdot5^n+1&5^n-1\\ 3\cdot5^n-3&5^n+3 \end{pmatrix}. \]

提示Solution

For \(n=1\), the right-hand side is

\[ \frac14\begin{pmatrix}16&4\\12&8\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\3&2\end{pmatrix}=A. \]

Assume the formula holds for \(n=k\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} A^{k+1} &=A^kA\\ &=\frac14 \begin{pmatrix} 3\cdot5^k+1&5^k-1\\ 3\cdot5^k-3&5^k+3 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}4&1\\3&2\end{pmatrix}\\ &=\frac14 \begin{pmatrix} 15\cdot5^k+1&5\cdot5^k-1\\ 15\cdot5^k-3&5\cdot5^k+3 \end{pmatrix}\\ &=\frac14 \begin{pmatrix} 3\cdot5^{k+1}+1&5^{k+1}-1\\ 3\cdot5^{k+1}-3&5^{k+1}+3 \end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the target for \(n=k+1\). Hence the formula holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记Exercise 2E, Question 10

It is given that \(u_r=r\cdot r!\) for \(r=1,2,3,\ldots\) and

\[ S_n=u_1+u_2+\cdots+u_n. \]

  1. Write down the values of \(2!-S_1\), \(3!-S_2\), \(4!-S_3\) and \(5!-S_4\).

  2. Conjecture a formula for \(S_n\).

  3. Prove the formula by mathematical induction for every positive integer \(n\).

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Question 3, October/November 2014.

提示Answer

\[ S_1=1, \; S_2=1+4=5, \; S_3=5+18=23, \; S_4=23+96=119. \]

Hence

\[ 2!-S_1=3!-S_2=4!-S_3=5!-S_4=1. \]

  1. The conjecture is

\[ \boxed{S_n=(n+1)!-1}. \]

  1. For \(n=1\), \(S_1=1\) and \(2!-1=1\), so the formula is true.

Assume that for \(n=k\),

\[ S_k=(k+1)!-1. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} S_{k+1} &=S_k+(k+1)(k+1)!\\ &=(k+1)!-1+(k+1)(k+1)!\\ &=(k+1)![1+(k+1)]-1\\ &=(k+2)!-1. \end{aligned} \]

This is the formula for \(n=k+1\). Therefore \(S_n=(n+1)!-1\) for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记Exercise 2E, Question 11

It is given that

\[ y=(1+x)^2\ln(1+x). \]

  1. Find \(d^3y/dx^3\).

  2. Prove by mathematical induction that, for every integer \(n\ge3\),

\[ \frac{d^ny}{dx^n} =(-1)^{n-1}\frac{2(n-3)!}{(1+x)^{n-2}}. \]

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Question 5, October/November 2015.

提示Answer
  1. Put \(v=1+x\). Then

\[ y'=2v\ln v+v, \]

\[ y''=2\ln v+3, \]

and therefore

\[ \boxed{y'''=\frac2{1+x}}. \]

  1. The result for \(n=3\) gives

\[ (-1)^2\frac{2(0)!}{1+x}=\frac2{1+x}, \]

which agrees with part (i).

Assume that for some \(k\ge3\),

\[ \frac{d^ky}{dx^k}=(-1)^{k-1}2(k-3)!(1+x)^{-(k-2)}. \]

Differentiate once:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}y}{dx^{k+1}} &=(-1)^{k-1}2(k-3)![-(k-2)](1+x)^{-(k-1)}\\ &=(-1)^k\frac{2(k-2)!}{(1+x)^{k-1}}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the proposed result with \(n=k+1\). Hence the formula holds for every integer \(n\ge3\) by mathematical induction.

注记Exercise 2E, Question 12

Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^n}{dx^n}(e^x\sin x) =2^{n/2}e^x\sin\left(x+\frac{n\pi}{4}\right). \]

Source: 9231 Paper 11 Question 3, October/November 2011.

提示Answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ \frac d{dx}(e^x\sin x)=e^x(\sin x+\cos x) =\sqrt2e^x\sin\left(x+\frac\pi4\right), \]

so the formula is true.

Assume that for \(n=k\),

\[ \frac{d^k}{dx^k}(e^x\sin x) =2^{k/2}e^x\sin\left(x+\frac{k\pi}{4}\right). \]

Let \(\theta=x+k\pi/4\). Differentiating gives

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}}{dx^{k+1}}(e^x\sin x) &=2^{k/2}e^x(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)\\ &=2^{k/2}\sqrt2e^x\sin\left(\theta+\frac\pi4\right)\\ &=2^{(k+1)/2}e^x\sin\left(x+\frac{(k+1)\pi}{4}\right). \end{aligned} \]

This is the target for \(n=k+1\). Therefore the formula holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

\[ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^m=\frac1{1-z}. \]

注记May/June 2019, Paper 13, Question 1

Prove by mathematical induction that \(3^{3n}-1\) is divisible by \(13\) for every positive integer \(n\). [5]

Source: 9231/13/M/J/19 Q1.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ 3^3-1=26=2\cdot13. \]

Assume that \(3^{3k}-1=13m\) for some integer \(m\). Then

注记October/November 2019, Paper 11/12/13, Question 2

It is given that \(y=\ln(ax+1)\), where \(a>0\). Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^ny}{dx^n} =(-1)^{n-1}\frac{(n-1)!a^n}{(ax+1)^n}. \]

[6]

Sources: 9231/11/O/N/19 Q2; 9231/12/O/N/19 Q2; 9231/13/O/N/19 Q2.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ y'=\frac{a}{ax+1}, \]

which agrees with the formula.

Assume that

\[ \frac{d^ky}{dx^k} =(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!a^k(ax+1)^{-k}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}y}{dx^{k+1}} &=(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!a^k(-k)a(ax+1)^{-k-1}\\ &=(-1)^k\frac{k!a^{k+1}}{(ax+1)^{k+1}}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the formula for \(n=k+1\). Hence it holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记May/June 2020, Paper 11/12, Question 6

Let

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

  1. The transformation represented by \(A^{-1}\) maps a triangle of area \(30\text{ cm}^2\) onto one of area \(d\text{ cm}^2\). Find \(d\). [3]

  2. Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ A^n=\begin{pmatrix}2^n&0\\2^n-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

[5]

Sources: 9231/11/M/J/20 Q6; 9231/12/M/J/20 Q6.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer
  1. Since \(\det A=2\),

\[ |\det A^{-1}|=\frac12. \]

Thus \(\boxed{d=15}\).

  1. For \(n=1\), the proposed expression is \(A\).

Assume that

\[ A^k=\begin{pmatrix}2^k&0\\2^k-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} A^{k+1} &=A^kA\\ &=\begin{pmatrix}2^k&0\\2^k-1&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\\ &=\begin{pmatrix}2^{k+1}&0\\2(2^k-1)+1&1\end{pmatrix}\\ &=\begin{pmatrix}2^{k+1}&0\\2^{k+1}-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the formula for \(n=k+1\). Hence it holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记May/June 2020, Paper 13, Question 2

The sequence satisfies \(u_1=1\) and

\[ u_{n+1}=2u_n+1. \]

  1. Prove by induction that \(u_n=2^n-1\) for every positive integer \(n\). [5]

  2. Deduce that \(u_{2n}\) is divisible by \(u_n\) for \(n\ge1\). [2]

Source: 9231/13/M/J/20 Q2.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer
  1. For \(n=1\), \(2^1-1=1=u_1\).

Assume that \(u_k=2^k-1\). Then

\[ u_{k+1}=2u_k+1=2(2^k-1)+1=2^{k+1}-1. \]

Thus the result holds for \(k+1\), and hence for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

\[ u_{2n}=2^{2n}-1=(2^n-1)(2^n+1)=u_n(2^n+1). \]

Since \(2^n+1\) is an integer, \(\boxed{u_n\mid u_{2n}}\).

注记October/November 2020, Paper 11/13, Question 5

Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^{2n-1}}{dx^{2n-1}}(x\sin x) =(-1)^{n-1}\{x\cos x+(2n-1)\sin x\}. \]

[7]

Sources: 9231/11/O/N/20 Q5; 9231/13/O/N/20 Q5.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ \frac d{dx}(x\sin x)=x\cos x+\sin x, \]

which is the proposed expression.

Assume that

\[ \frac{d^{2k-1}}{dx^{2k-1}}(x\sin x) =(-1)^{k-1}\{x\cos x+(2k-1)\sin x\}. \]

Differentiate twice. First,

\[ \frac{d^{2k}}{dx^{2k}}(x\sin x) =(-1)^{k-1}\{-x\sin x+2k\cos x\}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{2k+1}}{dx^{2k+1}}(x\sin x) &=(-1)^{k-1}\{-x\cos x-(2k+1)\sin x\}\\ &=(-1)^k\{x\cos x+(2k+1)\sin x\}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the target for \(n=k+1\). Hence the formula holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记October/November 2020, Paper 12, Question 2

Prove by mathematical induction that \(7^{2n}-1\) is divisible by \(12\) for every positive integer \(n\). [5]

Source: 9231/12/O/N/20 Q2.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ 7^2-1=48=4\cdot12. \]

Assume that \(7^{2k}-1=12m\) for some integer \(m\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} 7^{2(k+1)}-1 &=49\cdot7^{2k}-1\\ &=49(7^{2k}-1)+48\\ &=12(49m+4). \end{aligned} \]

Thus the expression is divisible by \(12\) for \(n=k+1\). Hence the result follows for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记May/June 2021, Paper 11/12, Question 1

Prove by mathematical induction that \(2^{4n}+31^n-2\) is divisible by \(15\) for every positive integer \(n\). [6]

Sources: 9231/11/M/J/21 Q1; 9231/12/M/J/21 Q1.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ 2^4+31-2=45=3\cdot15. \]

Assume that \(2^{4k}+31^k-2=15m\) for some integer \(m\). Then

\[ \begin{aligned} 2^{4(k+1)}+31^{k+1}-2 &=(15+1)2^{4k}+(30+1)31^k-2\\ &=(2^{4k}+31^k-2)+15\cdot2^{4k}+30\cdot31^k\\ &=15\{m+2^{4k}+2\cdot31^k\}. \end{aligned} \]

The bracket is an integer. Hence the expression is divisible by \(15\) for \(n=k+1\), and therefore for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{n}(5r^4+r^2) =\frac12n^2(n+1)^2(2n+1). \]

The sequence satisfies \(a_1=1\) and

\[ a_{n+1}=\left(a_n+\frac1{a_n}\right)^3. \]

  1. Prove by mathematical induction that

\[ \ln a_n\ge3^{n-1}\ln2 \]

for every integer \(n\ge2\). [6]

  1. Show that

\[ \ln a_{n+1}-\ln a_n>3^{n-1}\ln4 \]

注记October/November 2021, Paper 12, Question 2

It is given that \(y=xe^{ax}\), where \(a\) is constant. Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^ny}{dx^n}=(a^nx+na^{n-1})e^{ax}. \]

[6]

Source: 9231/12/O/N/21 Q2.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ y'=(ax+1)e^{ax}, \]

which agrees with the formula.

Assume

\[ \frac{d^ky}{dx^k}=(a^kx+ka^{k-1})e^{ax}. \]

Then

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}y}{dx^{k+1}} &=a(a^kx+ka^{k-1})e^{ax}+a^ke^{ax}\\ &=\{a^{k+1}x+(k+1)a^k\}e^{ax}. \end{aligned} \]

This is the target for \(n=k+1\). Hence the result holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

\[ u_{n+1}=\frac{u_n^2+u_n+12}{2u_n}. \]

  1. By considering \(u_{n+1}-4\), prove by mathematical induction that \(u_n>4\) for every positive integer \(n\). [5]

  2. Show that \(u_{n+1}<u_n\) for \(n\ge1\). [3]

提示Mark-scheme-style answer
  1. The base case \(u_1>4\) is given.

Assume that \(u_k>4\). Since \(u_k\) is positive,

\[ \begin{aligned} u_{k+1}-4 &=\frac{u_k^2+u_k+12}{2u_k}-4\\ &=\frac{u_k^2-7u_k+12}{2u_k}\\ &=\frac{(u_k-3)(u_k-4)}{2u_k}>0. \end{aligned} \]

because \(u_n>4\). Hence \(\boxed{u_{n+1}<u_n}\).

For \(x\ne1\), prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ 1+2x+3x^2+\cdots+nx^{n-1} =\frac{1-(n+1)x^n+nx^{n+1}}{(1-x)^2}. \]

[6]

\[ \text{LHS}=1, \; \text{RHS}=\frac{1-2x+x^2}{(1-x)^2}=1. \]

This is the target for \(n=k+1\). Hence the identity holds for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

注记October/November 2024, Paper 11/13, Question 2

Prove by mathematical induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ \frac{d^n}{dx^n}(\tan^{-1}x)=P_n(x)(1+x^2)^{-n}, \]

where \(P_n(x)\) is a polynomial of degree \(n-1\). [6]

Sources: 9231/11/O/N/24 Q2; 9231/13/O/N/24 Q2.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\),

\[ \frac d{dx}(\tan^{-1}x)=(1+x^2)^{-1}. \]

Thus \(P_1(x)=1\), which has degree \(0=1-1\).

Assume that

\[ \frac{d^k}{dx^k}(\tan^{-1}x)=P_k(x)(1+x^2)^{-k}, \]

where \(P_k\) has degree \(k-1\). Differentiating,

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d^{k+1}}{dx^{k+1}}(\tan^{-1}x) &=P_k'(x)(1+x^2)^{-k}-2kxP_k(x)(1+x^2)^{-k-1}\\ &=\{(1+x^2)P_k'(x)-2kxP_k(x)\}(1+x^2)^{-k-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Define

\[ P_{k+1}(x)=(1+x^2)P_k'(x)-2kxP_k(x). \]

Both terms have degree at most \(k\), and the leading coefficient is non-zero, so \(P_{k+1}\) has degree \(k\). This is the required form for \(n=k+1\). Hence the result follows for every positive integer \(n\) by mathematical induction.

Prove by mathematical induction that

\[ \left(\frac65\right)^n\ge1+\frac n5 \]

for every positive integer \(n\). [5]

Source: 9231/14/O/N/25 Q1.

提示Mark-scheme-style answer

For \(n=1\), both sides equal \(6/5\).

\[ \left(\frac65\right)^k\ge1+\frac k5. \]
