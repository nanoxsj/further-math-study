1.5 Polar coordinates

坐标转换 · 极坐标曲线 · 极坐标面积

CIE 9231 Paper 1，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Further Pure Mathematics 1 Chapter 5。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 理解 Cartesian coordinates 与 polar coordinates 的关系，并在两种形式之间转换点和曲线方程；
  2. 按照 \(r\geqslant0\) 的约定，在指定的 \(\theta\) 区间内描绘简单极坐标曲线；
  3. 在图中标出对称性、与 initial line 的交点、曲线在 pole 附近的形状，以及 \(r\) 的最大值和最小值；
  4. 使用 \[A=\frac12\int r^2\,\mathrm d\theta\] 求简单极坐标区域的面积。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Polar coordinates of a point

点 \(P\) 的极坐标写成 \((r,\theta)\)：\(r\) 是 \(P\) 到 pole \(O\) 的距离，\(\theta\) 是从 initial line 逆时针量到 \(OP\) 的角，通常用弧度表示。

同一点有无限多种表示：

\[ (r,\theta)\equiv(r,\theta+2k\pi),\; k\in\mathbb Z. \]

本教材的 principal polar coordinates 满足

\[ r>0,\; -\pi<\theta\leqslant\pi. \]

在 pole 处 \(r=0\)，而 \(\theta\) 没有唯一值。

1.2 Converting points and equations

两种坐标之间的基本关系是

\[ x=r\cos\theta,\; y=r\sin\theta, \; r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\; \tan\theta=\frac yx. \]

由 Cartesian coordinates 求 \(\theta\) 时，必须结合点所在象限选择正确的角，不能只使用计算器给出的 \(\arctan(y/x)\)。

转换曲线方程时常用

\[ r^2=x^2+y^2,\; r\cos\theta=x,\; r\sin\theta=y. \]

如果极坐标式中仍含 \(\sin\theta\) 或 \(\cos\theta\)，可乘以适当的 \(r\) 的幂，使它们与 \(x\)、\(y\) 配对。平方消去根号时，应保留原极坐标方程所给的 \(r\) 与 \(\theta\) 范围。

1.3 Sketching a polar curve

作图时依次检查：

  1. Range of \(\theta\) and the convention \(r\geqslant0\)：先确定题目要求的角度范围，再排除使 \(r<0\) 的部分；
  2. Pole：解 \(r=0\)，确定曲线从哪些方向到达或离开 pole；
  3. Initial line：检查 \(\theta=0\)，以及区间内与 initial line 同方向的角；
  4. Symmetry：比较 \(r(-\theta)\)、\(r(\pi-\theta)\) 或观察函数的周期；
  5. Least and greatest values of \(r\)：由三角函数范围或微分求极值；
  6. Direction and shape：取少量关键角，按 \(\theta\) 增大的方向连接。
注记Convention used in this course

若公式给出 \(r<0\)，本课程不把点画到相反方向，也不以虚线延伸；只绘制 \(r\geqslant0\) 的部分。

1.4 Symmetry tests

以下代换可帮助判断对称性：

\[ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{test} & \text{possible symmetry}\\ \hline \theta\mapsto-\theta & \text{initial line}\\ \theta\mapsto\pi-\theta & \theta=\frac\pi2\\ \theta\mapsto\theta+\pi & \text{pole} \end{array} \]

即使方程形式在代换后不完全相同，也可根据给定区间、周期和关键点确认实际图形的对称性。

1.5 Distances from lines

点 \((r,\theta)\) 到 initial line 的垂直距离为

\[ |y|=|r\sin\theta|. \]

在第一象限内可直接使用 \(r\sin\theta\)。点到半直线 \(\theta=\alpha\) 所在直线的垂直距离为

\[ |r\sin(\theta-\alpha)|. \]

求最远点时，应对相应距离函数微分，并检查端点以及区间内部的 stationary points。

1.6 Intersections of polar curves

在共同的 \(\theta\) 范围内令两个 \(r\) 表达式相等。还必须单独检查 pole，因为不同的 \(\theta\) 值都可能表示 \(r=0\) 的同一点。

若一条曲线是直线 \(r\sin\theta=c\)，它的 Cartesian equation 是 \(y=c\)；这有时能帮助判断交点个数和位置。

1.7 Area enclosed by a polar curve

曲线 \(r=f(\theta)\)、半直线 \(\theta=\alpha\) 与 \(\theta=\beta\) 所围区域的面积为

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,\mathrm d\theta}. \]

选择上下限前，应先由 \(r=0\)、曲线交点或题目给出的半直线确定边界。若图形关于某条直线对称，可只积一半再乘以 \(2\)。

两条曲线之间的面积通常写成

\[ A=\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta \left(r_{\text{outer}}^2-r_{\text{inner}}^2\right)\,\mathrm d\theta. \]

2. Textbook Examples

Example 5.1 · Pages 118–119

(i) Find the Cartesian coordinates of:

\[ \left(4,\frac{2\pi}{3}\right),\; \left(12,-\frac\pi6\right). \]

(ii) Find the principal polar coordinates of:

\[ (-\sqrt3,1),\; (4,-4). \]

Show solution

(i) Using \(x=r\cos\theta\) and \(y=r\sin\theta\),

\[ \begin{aligned} x&=4\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}=-2,& y&=4\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}=2\sqrt3,\\ x&=12\cos\left(-\frac\pi6\right)=6\sqrt3,& y&=12\sin\left(-\frac\pi6\right)=-6. \end{aligned} \]

Hence the Cartesian coordinates are

\[ \boxed{(-2,2\sqrt3)},\; \boxed{(6\sqrt3,-6)}. \]

(ii) For \((-\sqrt3,1)\),

\[ r=\sqrt{(-\sqrt3)^2+1^2}=2. \]

The point is in quadrant II and its reference angle is \(\pi/6\), so

\[ \boxed{\left(2,\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)}. \]

For \((4,-4)\),

\[ r=\sqrt{4^2+(-4)^2}=4\sqrt2. \]

The point is in quadrant IV, so the principal angle is \(-\pi/4\). Thus

\[ \boxed{\left(4\sqrt2,-\frac\pi4\right)}. \]

Example 5.2 · Pages 120–121

Find polar equations of the curves

\[ \text{(i) }x^2-y^2=9,\; \text{(ii) }y=x+4. \]

Show solution

(i) Substitute \(x=r\cos\theta\) and \(y=r\sin\theta\):

\[ r^2(\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta)=9. \]

Since \(\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta=\cos2\theta\),

\[ \boxed{r^2=9\sec2\theta}. \]

(ii) Substitution gives

\[ r\sin\theta=r\cos\theta+4, \]

so

\[ r(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)=4. \]

Using

\[ \sin\theta-\cos\theta =\sqrt2\sin\left(\theta-\frac\pi4\right), \]

the required equation is

\[ \boxed{r=2\sqrt2\,\operatorname{cosec}\left(\theta-\frac\pi4\right)}. \]

Example 5.3 · Page 121

Find Cartesian equations of the curves

\[ \text{(i) }r=5,\; \text{(ii) }r^2=4\sin\theta. \]

Show solution

(i) Since \(r^2=x^2+y^2\),

\[ \boxed{x^2+y^2=25}. \]

(ii) Multiply by \(r\):

\[ r^3=4r\sin\theta=4y. \]

Squaring and using \(r^2=x^2+y^2\) gives

\[ r^6=16y^2, \]

and hence

\[ \boxed{(x^2+y^2)^3=16y^2}. \]

Example 5.4 · Page 124

Sketch the graphs of

\[ \text{(i) }r=3,\; \text{(ii) }\theta=\frac\pi4. \]

Show solution

For \(r=3\), every point is a distance \(3\) from the pole, so the graph is a circle with centre \(O\) and radius \(3\). It crosses the initial line at \((3,0)\).

For \(\theta=\pi/4\), the angle is fixed while \(r\geqslant0\) varies. The graph is therefore a half-line from the pole at angle \(\pi/4\); it is not the complete Cartesian line \(y=x\).

Example 5.5 · Page 125

A curve is defined by

\[ r=5\cos^2\theta. \]

(i) Create a table of values for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi\).

(ii) Plot the curve for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant2\pi\).

(iii) State the least and greatest values of \(r\) and the values of \(\theta\) at which they occur.

Show solution

At intervals of \(\pi/12\) from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\):

\[ \begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr} \theta&0&\frac\pi{12}&\frac\pi6&\frac\pi4&\frac\pi3&\frac{5\pi}{12}&\frac\pi2\\ \hline r&5&4.67&3.75&2.50&1.25&0.34&0 \end{array} \]

The values are reflected between \(\pi/2\) and \(\pi\) and repeat with period \(\pi\). The initial line is an axis of symmetry.

Since \(0\leqslant\cos^2\theta\leqslant1\),

\[ \boxed{r_{\max}=5\text{ at }\theta=0,\pi}, \]

and

\[ \boxed{r_{\min}=0\text{ at }\theta=\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}{2}}. \]

Example 5.6 · Page 126

Find the least and greatest values of \(r\) for

\[ r=3+2\cos4\theta \]

and the values of \(\theta\) at which they occur. Hence sketch the graph.

Show solution

The least value is

\[ 3-2=1, \]

which occurs when \(\cos4\theta=-1\). For \(-\pi<\theta\leqslant\pi\), this gives

\[ \theta=\pm\frac\pi4,\ \pm\frac{3\pi}{4}. \]

The greatest value is

\[ 3+2=5, \]

which occurs when \(\cos4\theta=1\), including

\[ \theta=0,\ \pm\frac\pi2,\ \pi. \]

The function has period \(\pi/2\), so the graph repeats in each quadrant and has rotational symmetry of order \(4\). It never reaches the pole because \(r\geqslant1\).

Example 5.7 · Pages 126–129

A curve is given by

\[ r=\theta(\pi-\theta). \]

(i) Plot the curve for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi\), where \(r\) is non-negative.

(ii) Create tables of values for \(\pi\leqslant\theta\leqslant3\pi/2\) and \(-\pi/2\leqslant\theta\leqslant0\).

(iii) Sketch the curve for \(-\pi/2\leqslant\theta\leqslant3\pi/2\) using the convention for this course.

Show solution

For \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi\), \(r\geqslant0\). The curve starts at the pole when \(\theta=0\), reaches its greatest radius at \(\theta=\pi/2\), and returns to the pole at \(\theta=\pi\):

\[ r_{\max}=\frac\pi2\left(\pi-\frac\pi2\right)=\frac{\pi^2}{4}. \]

Outside this interval, \(\theta(\pi-\theta)<0\). For instance,

\[ \begin{array}{c|rrrr} \theta&\pi&\frac{7\pi}{6}&\frac{4\pi}{3}&\frac{3\pi}{2}\\ \hline r&0&-\frac{7\pi^2}{36}&-\frac{4\pi^2}{9}&-\frac{3\pi^2}{4} \end{array} \]

and

\[ \begin{array}{c|rrrr} \theta&-\frac\pi2&-\frac\pi3&-\frac\pi6&0\\ \hline r&-\frac{3\pi^2}{4}&-\frac{4\pi^2}{9}&-\frac{7\pi^2}{36}&0. \end{array} \]

Under the convention \(r\geqslant0\), these negative-radius portions are omitted. The required graph therefore consists only of the loop traced for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi\).

Example 5.8 · Pages 132–133

The curve has equation

\[ r=1+2\cos\theta. \]

Find the area enclosed, using the convention \(r\geqslant0\).

Show solution

The curve reaches the pole when

\[ 1+2\cos\theta=0 \Longrightarrow \cos\theta=-\frac12. \]

The relevant limiting angles are \(\theta=\pm2\pi/3\). By symmetry about the initial line,

\[ A=2\left(\frac12\int_0^{2\pi/3}(1+2\cos\theta)^2\,\mathrm d\theta\right). \]

Using \(4\cos^2\theta=2+2\cos2\theta\),

\[ \begin{aligned} A &=\int_0^{2\pi/3}(3+4\cos\theta+2\cos2\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta\\ &=\left[3\theta+4\sin\theta+\sin2\theta\right]_0^{2\pi/3}\\ &=2\pi+2\sqrt3-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{A=2\pi+\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}}. \]

Exercise 5B · Question 9 · Page 131

The curves \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) have polar equations

\[ C_1:r=\frac12,\; 0\leqslant\theta\leqslant2\pi, \]

and

\[ C_2:r=\sin\frac\theta2,\; 0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi. \]

Find the polar coordinates of the point of intersection of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\). Sketch \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) on the same diagram.

Show solution

At an intersection away from the pole, the two curves have the same \(r\) and \(\theta\):

\[ \sin\frac\theta2=\frac12. \]

Since \(0\leqslant\theta/2\leqslant\pi/2\),

\[ \frac\theta2=\frac\pi6 \Longrightarrow \theta=\frac\pi3. \]

Thus the point of intersection is

\[ \boxed{\left(\frac12,\frac\pi3\right)}. \]

\(C_1\) is the circle with centre at the pole and radius \(1/2\). The curve \(C_2\) starts at the pole, passes through the stated intersection and reaches \((-1,0)\) when \(\theta=\pi\).

Source: Cambridge International 9231/11/M/J/2015, Q5 (part question).

Exercise 5C · Question 9 · Page 134

The curve \(C\) has polar equation

\[ r=a(1+\sin\theta),\; 0\leqslant\theta<2\pi, \]

where \(a\) is a positive constant. Draw a sketch of \(C\).

Find the exact area of the region enclosed by \(C\) and the half-lines \(\theta=\pi/3\) and \(\theta=2\pi/3\).

Show solution

The curve is symmetric about \(\theta=\pi/2\). It has

\[ r=2a\text{ at }\theta=\frac\pi2,\; r=0\text{ at }\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}, \]

and meets the initial line at \(r=a\).

The required area is

\[ A=\frac12a^2\int_{\pi/3}^{2\pi/3}(1+\sin\theta)^2\,\mathrm d\theta. \]

Since

\[ (1+\sin\theta)^2 =\frac32+2\sin\theta-\frac12\cos2\theta, \]

we obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} A &=\frac{a^2}{2} \left[ \frac32\theta-2\cos\theta-\frac14\sin2\theta \right]_{\pi/3}^{2\pi/3}\\ &=\frac{a^2}{2}left(\frac\pi2+2+\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\right). \end{aligned} \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{A=\frac{a^2}{8}\left(2\pi+8+\sqrt3\right)}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/11/M/J/2014, Q5.

Exercise 5C · Question 10 · Page 134

The curve \(C\) has polar equation

\[ r=2+2\cos\theta,\; 0\leqslant\theta<\pi. \]

Sketch \(C\). Find the area of the region \(R\) enclosed by \(C\) and the initial line.

The half-line \(\theta=\pi/5\) divides \(R\) into two parts. Find the area of each part, correct to \(3\) decimal places.

Show solution

The curve starts at \((4,0)\) and reaches the pole at \(\theta=\pi\). The required region is the part above the initial line.

\[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{area}(R) &=\frac12\int_0^\pi(2+2\cos\theta)^2\,\mathrm d\theta\\ &=\int_0^\pi(3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta\\ &=\left[3\theta+4\sin\theta+\frac12\sin2\theta\right]_0^\pi\\ &=\boxed{3\pi}. \end{aligned} \]

The area between \(\theta=0\) and \(\theta=\pi/5\) is

\[ \begin{aligned} A_1 &=\left[3\theta+4\sin\theta+\frac12\sin2\theta\right]_0^{\pi/5}\\ &=4.71163\ldots\\ &=\boxed{4.712}. \end{aligned} \]

The remaining area is

\[ A_2=3\pi-A_1=4.71315\ldots=\boxed{4.713}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/11/M/J/2012, Q4.

2019 May/June · 9231/11 & 9231/12 · Question 11 EITHER · 14 marks

\(C_1\) has equation \(r^2=2\theta\) for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2\). The point \(P\) is furthest from the line \(\theta=\pi/2\).

(i) Show that \(2\theta\tan\theta=1\) at \(P\), and verify a root between \(0.6\) and \(0.7\). [5]

\(C_2\) has equation \(r^2=\theta\sec^2\theta\) for \(0\leqslant\theta<\pi/2\). The curves meet at the pole and at \(Q\).

(ii) Find the exact value of \(\theta\) at \(Q\). [2]

(iii) Sketch \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) together. [2]

(iv) Find exactly the area enclosed by the curves. [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(i) Distance from \(\theta=\pi/2\) is \(x=r\cos\theta=(2\theta)^{1/2}\cos\theta\). Hence

\[ \frac{dx}{d\theta}=(2\theta)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\cos\theta}{2\theta}-\sin\theta\right)=0 \Longrightarrow \boxed{2\theta\tan\theta=1}. \]

For \(f(\theta)=2\theta\tan\theta-1\),

\[f(0.6)=-0.179<0,\; f(0.7)=0.179>0,\]

so a root lies in \((0.6,0.7)\).

(ii) Away from the pole,

\[2\theta=\theta\sec^2\theta\Longrightarrow\cos^2\theta=\frac12,\]

so \(\boxed{\theta=\pi/4}\).

(iv) \(C_1\) is outside \(C_2\) for \(0<\theta<\pi/4\), so

\[ \begin{aligned} A&=\frac12\int_0^{\pi/4}\left(2\theta-\theta\sec^2\theta\right)d\theta\\ &=\boxed{\frac{\pi^2}{32}-\frac\pi8+\frac14\ln2}. \end{aligned} \]

Sources: 9231/11/M/J/2019 Q11 EITHER; 9231/12/M/J/2019 Q11 EITHER.

2019 May/June · 9231/13 · Question 2 · 7 marks

\(C\) has equation \(r^2=\ln(1+\theta)\) for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant2\pi\).

(i) Sketch \(C\). [2]

(ii) Find exactly the area bounded by \(C\) and the initial line. [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

The curve starts at the pole, spirals anticlockwise with increasing \(r\), and returns to the initial line at \(\theta=2\pi\); it is tangent to the initial line at the pole.

\[ \begin{aligned} A&=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}\ln(1+\theta)d\theta\\ &=\frac12\int_1^{2\pi+1}\ln u\,du\\ &=\frac12[u\ln u-u]_1^{2\pi+1}\\ &=\boxed{\left(\pi+\frac12\right)\ln(2\pi+1)-\pi}. \end{aligned} \]

Source: 9231/13/M/J/2019 Q2.

2019 October/November · 9231/11, /12 & /13 · Question 11 EITHER · 14 marks

For \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2\),

\[C_1:r=2(e^\theta+e^{-\theta}),\; C_2:r=e^{2\theta}-e^{-2\theta}.\]

The curves meet at \(P\) where \(\theta=\alpha\).

(i) Show that \(e^{2\alpha}-2e^\alpha-1=0\), find \(\alpha\) exactly and show that \(r=4\sqrt2\) at \(P\). [6]

(ii) Sketch both curves. [3]

(iii) Find to \(3\) significant figures the area enclosed by the curves and initial line. [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

Equating radii and dividing by \(e^\alpha+e^{-\alpha}\) gives \(e^\alpha-e^{-\alpha}=2\), hence

\[e^{2\alpha}-2e^\alpha-1=0,\; e^\alpha=1+\sqrt2,\]

so

\[\boxed{\alpha=\ln(1+\sqrt2)},\; \boxed{r=4\sqrt2}.\]

The required area is

\[ \frac12\int_0^\alpha\left[4(e^\theta+e^{-\theta})^2-(e^{2\theta}-e^{-2\theta})^2\right]d\theta. \]

After expansion this equals

\[ \left[5\theta+e^{2\theta}-e^{-2\theta}-\frac18e^{4\theta}+\frac18e^{-4\theta}\right]_0^\alpha =5\alpha+\sqrt2. \]

Thus \(\boxed{A=5.82}\) to \(3\) significant figures.

Sources: 9231/11/O/N/2019 Q11 EITHER; 9231/12/O/N/2019 Q11 EITHER; 9231/13/O/N/2019 Q11 EITHER.

2020

2020 May/June · 9231/11 & /12 · Question 7 · 12 marks

\(C_1:r=\theta\cos\theta\) and \(C_2:r=\theta\sin\theta\), for \(0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2\).

(a) Show that the point of \(C_1\) furthest from \(\theta=\pi/2\) satisfies \(2\theta\tan\theta-1=0\), and verify a root in \((0.6,0.7)\). [5]

(b) Find the non-pole intersection. [2] (c) Sketch both curves. [3] (d) Find the enclosed area exactly. [2]

Show answer and mark scheme

\(x=r\cos\theta=\theta\cos^2\theta\), so

\[\frac{dx}{d\theta}=\cos^2\theta-2\theta\sin\theta\cos\theta=0 \Longrightarrow 2\theta\tan\theta-1=0.\]
