1.4 Matrices

矩阵运算 · 行列式与逆矩阵 · 平面变换 · 不变量

CIE 9231 Paper 1，依据 2026–2027 syllabus 与 Further Pure Mathematics 1 Chapters 1 and 6。
重要本节考纲要求

考生需要能够：

  1. 对不超过 \(3\times3\) 的矩阵进行加、减、乘运算，并识别零矩阵与单位矩阵；
  2. 判断方阵是 singular 还是 non-singular，计算 \(2\times2\)、\(3\times3\) 行列式和非奇异矩阵的逆；
  3. 使用 \((AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}\)，并推广到多个矩阵的乘积；
  4. 用 \(2\times2\) 矩阵表示旋转、反射、放大、伸缩、剪切等平面变换；
  5. 理解复合变换的顺序：\(AB\) 表示先作 \(B\)，再作 \(A\)；
  6. 使用 \(|\det M|\) 求面积伸缩因子；
  7. 求矩阵变换下的不动点与不变直线。

学习路线

1. 教材知识点

1.1 Matrix operations

只有阶数相同的矩阵可以相加或相减。若 \(A\) 为 \(m\times n\)，\(B\) 为 \(n\times p\)，则 \(AB\) 有定义且为 \(m\times p\)。

矩阵乘法一般不满足交换律：

\[ AB\ne BA, \]

但满足结合律与分配律。单位矩阵 \(I\) 满足 \(AI=IA=A\)，零矩阵记作 \(O\)。

1.2 Determinants and inverses

对于

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}, \]

\[ \det A=ad-bc. \]

若 \(\det A=0\)，则 \(A\) singular，不存在逆矩阵；若 \(\det A\ne0\)，则

\[ A^{-1}=\frac1{ad-bc}\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}. \]

对 \(3\times3\) 矩阵可按任意一行或一列展开行列式；逆矩阵可由余子式、代数余子式和伴随矩阵求得：

\[ A^{-1}=\frac1{\det A}\operatorname{adj}A. \]

1.3 Products and inverse order

若 \(A,B\) 均非奇异，

\[ (AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}. \]

右侧顺序必须反转。对三个矩阵，

\[ (ABC)^{-1}=C^{-1}B^{-1}A^{-1}. \]

1.4 Matrices as transformations

若

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}, \]

则

\[ \begin{pmatrix}x'\\y'\end{pmatrix} =M\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}. \]

矩阵第一列是 \(\binom10\) 的像，第二列是 \(\binom01\) 的像。常用矩阵包括

\[ R_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}, \]

以及

\[ \begin{pmatrix}k&0\\0&k\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}1&h\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\k&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

1.5 Successive transformations and area

若先作 \(B\)、再作 \(A\)，复合矩阵为 \(AB\)。矩阵 \(M\) 所表示变换的面积伸缩因子为

\[ |\det M|. \]

行列式的符号还反映方向是否翻转。

1.6 Invariant points and lines

不动点 \(\mathbf{x}\) 满足

\[ M\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}, \]

即 \((M-I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf0\)。

若过原点的直线 \(y=mx\) 在变换后仍映到自身，则方向向量 \(\binom1m\) 的像必须仍与 \(\binom1m\) 平行。对于一般直线 \(y=mx+c\)，应把像点代回同一条直线并比较系数。

Multiply

\[ \begin{pmatrix}3&2\\-1&4\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \]

Example 1.4 · Page 16

Describe the transformations represented by

\[ \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}. \]

Show solution

The first matrix interchanges the coordinates, so it is reflection in \(y=x\). The second multiplies both coordinates by \(2\), so it is an enlargement of scale factor \(2\) about the origin.

Example 1.5 · Pages 16–18

Find the matrices representing:

  1. reflection in the \(x\)-axis;
  2. enlargement of scale factor \(3\) about the origin.
Show solution

Use the images of \(\binom10\) and \(\binom01\) as the columns:

\[ \boxed{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}}, \qquad \boxed{\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Example 1.6 · Pages 19–20

(i) Find the matrix for anticlockwise rotation through \(\theta\) about the origin.

(ii) Hence give the matrix for an anticlockwise rotation through \(60^\circ\).

Show solution

\[ \boxed{R_\theta= \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Thus

\[ \boxed{R_{60^\circ}= \begin{pmatrix}\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[2pt]\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Example 1.7 · Pages 21–22

The rectangle has vertices \(A(-1,2)\), \(B(1,2)\), \(C(1,-1)\) and \(D(-1,-1)\). Find its image under

\[ \begin{pmatrix}1&3\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Show solution

Multiplying the coordinate matrix gives

\[ \begin{pmatrix}1&3\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}-1&1&1&-1\\2&2&-1&-1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}5&7&-2&-4\\2&2&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{A'(5,2),\ B'(7,2),\ C'(-2,-1),\ D'(-4,-1)}. \]

Example 1.8 · Pages 23–24

In a shear \(S\), the \(y\)-axis is fixed and \((1,0)\) maps to \((1,5)\). Find the matrix of \(S\).

Show solution

The images of the basis vectors are \(\binom15\) and \(\binom01\), so

\[ \boxed{S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\5&1\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Example 1.9 · Pages 29–30

Let \(A,B,C\) represent anticlockwise rotations through \(135^\circ\), \(45^\circ\), \(90^\circ\), respectively.

  1. Write down the matrices.
  2. Verify \(A=BC\).
  3. Calculate \(B^3\) and comment.
Show solution

Using \(R_\theta\) gives the three matrices. Since successive anticlockwise rotations add their angles,

\[ \boxed{BC=R_{45^\circ}R_{90^\circ}=R_{135^\circ}=A}, \]

and similarly

\[ \boxed{B^3=R_{135^\circ}=A}. \]

Example 1.10 · Page 33

Let

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

(i) Show that \((5,5)\) is invariant. (ii) Find all invariant points.

Show solution

Direct multiplication maps \(\binom55\) to itself. In general,

\[ M\binom{x}{y}=\binom{x}{y} \]

gives \(2x-y=x\) and \(x=y\). Hence every invariant point lies on

\[ \boxed{y=x}. \]

Example 1.11 · Pages 34–35

Find the invariant lines of

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}5&1\\2&4\end{pmatrix}. \]

Show solution

Put \(y=mx+c\) and require the image point to satisfy the same equation. Comparing coefficients gives

\[ m^2+m-2=0, \qquad (m-3)c=0. \]

Therefore \(c=0\) and

\[ \boxed{y=x\quad\text{or}\quad y=-2x}. \]

Example 6.1 · Page 137

A shape of area \(8\text{ cm}^2\) is transformed by

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\3&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

Find the image area.

Show solution

\(\det M=6\), so the area scale factor is \(6\). The image area is

\[ \boxed{48\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Example 6.2 · Pages 137–138

The unit square is transformed by

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\4&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Find its image vertices, \(\det M\), and the image area.

Show solution

The four vertices map to \((0,0),(2,4),(5,5),(3,1)\). Also

\[ \det M=2-12=-10. \]

Hence the image area is \(\boxed{10}\) square units.

Example 6.3 · Page 138

Given

\[ P=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}, \]

find \(\det P\), \(\det Q\), and \(\det(PQ)\).

Show solution

\[ \det P=2,\qquad \det Q=3, \]

and

\[ PQ=\begin{pmatrix}5&4\\1&2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(PQ)=6. \]

Thus \(\boxed{\det(PQ)=\det P\det Q}\).

Example 6.4 · Page 142

Write down matrices \(A,B\) for rotations through \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise and clockwise, and find \(AB\).

Show solution

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

Then

\[ \boxed{AB=I}, \]

so the two matrices represent inverse transformations.

Example 6.5 · Page 144

Let

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}11&3\\6&2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(i) Find \(A^{-1}\). (ii) If \(A\) maps \(P\) to \(Q(5,2)\), find \(P\).

Show solution

Since \(\det A=4\),

\[ \boxed{A^{-1}=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}2&-3\\-6&11\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Then

\[ A^{-1}\binom52=\binom1{-2}, \]

so \(\boxed{P=(1,-2)}\).

Example 6.6 · Page 148

Find the determinant of

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}3&0&-4\\7&2&-1\\-2&1&3\end{pmatrix}. \]

Show solution

Expanding along the top row,

\[ \det M =3\begin{vmatrix}2&-1\\1&3\end{vmatrix} -4\begin{vmatrix}7&2\\-2&1\end{vmatrix} =21-44. \]

Therefore \(\boxed{\det M=-23}\).

Example 6.7 · Pages 150–151

Find the inverse of

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}2&3&4\\2&-5&2\\-3&6&-3\end{pmatrix} \]

without a calculator.

Show solution

The determinant is \(-6\). The cofactor matrix is

\[ \begin{pmatrix}3&0&-3\\33&6&-21\\26&4&-16\end{pmatrix}. \]

Transpose and divide by \(-6\):

\[ \boxed{M^{-1}=\frac16 \begin{pmatrix}-3&-33&-26\\0&-6&-4\\3&21&16\end{pmatrix}}. \]

Multiplication verifies \(MM^{-1}=I\).

2020 May/June · 9231/11 & 9231/12 · Question 6 · 13 marks

Let

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

(a) The transformation represented by \(A^{-1}\) maps a triangle of area \(30\text{ cm}^2\) to a triangle of area \(d\text{ cm}^2\). Find \(d\). [3]

(b) Prove by induction that, for every positive integer \(n\),

\[ A^n=\begin{pmatrix}2^n&0\\2^n-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

[5]

Let \(B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\33&0\end{pmatrix}\).

(c) The line \(y=2x\) is invariant under the transformation represented by \(A^nB\). Find \(n\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Since

\[ \det A=2,\qquad \det(A^{-1})=(\det A)^{-1}=\frac12, \]

the area scale factor is \(\left|\det(A^{-1})\right|=\frac12\). Therefore

\[ \boxed{d=30\times\frac12=15}. \]

(b) For \(n=1\),

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}2^1&0\\2^1-1&1\end{pmatrix}, \]

so the statement is true initially. Assume that it is true for \(n=k\), where \(k\) is a positive integer:

\[ A^k=\begin{pmatrix}2^k&0\\2^k-1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Then

\[ A^{k+1}=\begin{pmatrix}2^k&0\\2^k-1&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}2^{k+1}&0\\2^{k+1}-1&1\end{pmatrix}, \]

Thus the statement is true for \(k+1\) whenever it is true for \(k\). Hence, by mathematical induction,

\[ \boxed{A^n=\begin{pmatrix}2^n&0\\2^n-1&1\end{pmatrix}} \]

for every positive integer \(n\).

(c) Using part (b),

\[ A^nB=\begin{pmatrix}2^n&0\\2^n+32&0\end{pmatrix}. \]

For a general point \((x,y)\),

\[ A^nB\binom xy =\binom{2^nx}{(2^n+32)x}. \]

The image has gradient \((2^n+32)/2^n\). Since the invariant line has gradient \(2\),

\[ \frac{2^n+32}{2^n}=2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^n=32 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{n=5}. \]

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/11/M/J/2020, Q6; 9231/12/M/J/2020, Q6.

2020 May/June · 9231/13 · Question 4 · 11 marks

Let

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}k&0&2\\0&-1&-1\\1&1&-k\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-3\\-1&3\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}-3&-1&1\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix}. \]

(a) Show that \(A\) is non-singular. [3]

It is given that

\[ CAB=\begin{pmatrix}-2&-\frac32\\-1&-\frac32\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Find \(k\). [3]

(c) Find the invariant lines through the origin of the transformation represented by \(CAB\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Expanding along the first row,

\[ \det A =k\begin{vmatrix}-1&-1\\1&-k\end{vmatrix} +2\begin{vmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{vmatrix} =k(k+1)+2 =k^2+k+2. \]

The discriminant of \(k^2+k+2\) is

\[ 1^2-4(1)(2)=-7<0. \]

Since its leading coefficient is positive, \(k^2+k+2>0\) for every real \(k\). Thus \(\det A\ne0\) and \(A\) is non-singular.

(b) First,

\[ AB=\begin{pmatrix}0&-3k\\1&-3\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}, \]

and hence

\[ CAB =\begin{pmatrix}-2&9k+3\\-1&-3k-3\end{pmatrix}. \]

Comparing, for example, the top-right entry with the given matrix,

\[ 9k+3=-\frac32 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{k=-\frac12}. \]

(c) The given \(CAB\) maps a point \((x,y)\) to

\[ \binom XY =\begin{pmatrix}-2&-\frac32\\-1&-\frac32\end{pmatrix} \binom xy =\binom{-2x-\frac32y}{-x-\frac32y}. \]

For the line \(y=mx\), substitute \(y=mx\) and require \(Y=mX\):

\[ -1-\frac32m=m\left(-2-\frac32m\right), \]

Thus

\[ 3m^2+m-2=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (3m-2)(m+1)=0. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{y=\frac23x\quad\text{or}\quad y=-x}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/13/M/J/2020, Q4.

2020 October/November · 9231/11 & 9231/13 · Question 1 · 9 marks

Let

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}, \]

where \(a,b>0\).

(a) State the two transformations represented by \(M\), in the correct order. [2]

The unit square is mapped by \(M\) onto parallelogram \(OPQR\).

(b) Find the matrix mapping \(OPQR\) back onto the unit square. [2]

(c) Given that the area of \(OPQR\) is \(2\text{ cm}^2\) and \(x+3y=0\) is invariant, find \(a\) and \(b\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) Reading the product from right to left, first apply a one-way stretch parallel to the \(x\)-axis with scale factor \(a\), then a shear with the \(x\)-axis fixed, taking \((0,1)\) to \((b,1)\).

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Since \(\det M=a\),

\[ \boxed{M^{-1}=\frac1a\begin{pmatrix}1&-b\\0&a\end{pmatrix}}. \]

(c) The unit square has area \(1\), so its image has area

\[ |\det M|=a. \]

As \(a>0\) and the given area is \(2\), \(\boxed{a=2}\). A general point on \(x+3y=0\) can be written as \((x,-x/3)\). Its image is

\[ \binom XY =\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \binom{x}{-x/3} =\binom{ax-\frac13bx}{-\frac13x}. \]

The image must again satisfy \(X+3Y=0\):

\[ ax-\frac13bx-x=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1=a-\frac b3. \]

Using \(a=2\) gives \(\boxed{b=3}\), so \(\boxed{a=2, b=3}\).

Sources: Cambridge International 9231/11/O/N/2020, Q1; 9231/13/O/N/2020, Q1.

2020 October/November · 9231/12 · Question 4 · 13 marks

Let

\[ A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[2pt]\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix}. \]

(a) Identify the transformation represented by \(A\). [1]

(b) Identify the transformation represented by \(B\). [2]

Triangle \(DEF\) is transformed by \(AB\) onto triangle \(PQR\).

(c) Show that the two triangles have the same area. [3]

(d) Find the matrix mapping \(PQR\) onto \(DEF\). [2]

(e) Find the invariant lines through the origin of the transformation represented by \(AB\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) \(A\) represents reflection in the line \(y=x\).

(b) Comparing \(B\) with the standard rotation matrix shows that it represents a rotation through \(60^\circ\) anticlockwise about the origin.

(c) Multiplying in the stated order,

\[ AB=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\\[2pt]\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(AB)=-1, \]

Therefore the area scale factor is \(|\det(AB)|=1\), so triangles \(DEF\) and \(PQR\) have equal area.

(d) Using the \(2\times2\) inverse formula,

\[ \boxed{(AB)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\\[2pt]\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\end{pmatrix}}. \]

(e) Under \(AB\),

\[ \binom XY =\binom{\frac{\sqrt3}{2}x+\frac12y}{\frac12x-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}y}. \]

Put \(y=mx\) and require \(Y=mX\):

\[ \frac12-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}m =m\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac12m\right). \]

Hence

\[ m^2+2\sqrt3m-1=0, \]

so \(m=-\sqrt3\pm2\). Therefore

\[ \boxed{y=(2-\sqrt3)x\quad\text{or}\quad y=-(2+\sqrt3)x}. \]

Source: Cambridge International 9231/12/O/N/2020, Q4.

2021 May/June · 9231/11 & 9231/12 · Question 4 · 14 marks

A rotation of \(60^\circ\) anticlockwise about the origin is followed by a one-way stretch in the \(x\)-direction with scale factor \(d\), where \(d\ne0\).

(a) Find the matrix \(M\) of the combined transformation. [4]

(b) The unit square is mapped by \(M\) onto a parallelogram of area \(\frac12d^2\). Show that \(d=2\). [2]

Let \(N\) satisfy

\[ MN=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\[2pt]\frac12&\frac12\end{pmatrix}. \]

(c) Find \(N\). [3]

(d) Find the invariant lines through the origin of the transformation represented by \(MN\). [5]

Show answer and mark scheme

(a) The rotation matrix is

\[ \begin{pmatrix}\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[2pt]\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix}, \]

and the following stretch has matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}d&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\). Hence, in the correct order,

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}d&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[2pt]\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\frac d2&-\frac{d\sqrt3}{2}\\[2pt]\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix}. \]

(b) Since

\[ \det M=\frac d4+\frac{3d}{4}=d, \]

the image area is \(|d|\). In the geometrical stretch context \(d>0\), so

\[ d=\frac12d^2. \]

As \(d\ne0\), \(\boxed{d=2}\).

(c) With \(d=2\),
