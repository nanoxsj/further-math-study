4.2 Inference using normal and t-distributions

Further Probability & Statistics · confidence intervals · t-tests · paired data

CIE 9231 Paper 4 inference using normal and t-distributions，依据 John du Feu Further Probability & Statistics。
重要本节考纲要求

本章使用样本资料对总体均值作估计和检验，包括 confidence intervals、one-sample t-tests、two-sample tests 和 paired-sample t-tests。答案中的每个小问均按 Show answer 展开，并按 Mark Scheme 分点书写。

1. 核心公式

For a normal population with unknown variance, \[t=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n},\qquad v=n-1.\]

A two-sided \(100(1-\alpha)\%\) confidence interval is \[\bar x\pm t_{1-\alpha/2,v}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}.\]

For paired data, form \(d_i=x_i-y_i\) and apply the one-sample procedure to the differences. For two independent large samples, \[z=\frac{\bar x-\bar y-(\mu_X-\mu_Y)_0}{\sqrt{s_X^2/n_X+s_Y^2/n_Y}}.\]

2. 教材 Examples

以下题目来自 John du Feu, Further Probability & Statistics, Chapter 2，题目保持英文。

Example 2.1 · Confidence interval for one mean

A bus company makes nine trial runs. The journey times, in minutes, are

\[89,\ 92,\ 95,\ 94,\ 88,\ 90,\ 92,\ 93,\ 91.\]

(i) Use these data to set up a 95% confidence interval for the mean journey time. You should assume that the journey times are normally distributed.

The railway service takes 95 minutes.

(ii) Does your confidence interval provide evidence that the journey time by bus is different from that by train?

Show answer

(i) \(n=9\), \(v=8\), \(\bar x=91.56\) and \(s=2.297\). Using \(t_{0.975,8}=2.306\), \[91.56\pm2.306\frac{2.297}{\sqrt9}=\boxed{[89.79,93.33]}.\]

(ii) \(95\) is outside the interval. Therefore there is sufficient evidence that the mean bus journey time differs from \(95\) minutes; the evidence indicates that it is less.

Example 2.2 · Paired-sample confidence interval

Twelve subjects support a 2 kg weight under two conditions. Their times, in seconds, are:

Subject A B C D E F G H I J K L
Group 61 71 72 53 71 43 85 72 82 54 70 73
Alone 43 72 81 35 56 39 63 66 38 60 74 52

Find a 90% confidence interval for the true difference between group times and alone times. You may assume that the differences are normally distributed. Does the result provide evidence that there is any difference in the population?

Show answer

The differences have \(\bar d=10.67\), \(s_d=15.24\), \(n=12\), \(v=11\). With \(t_{0.95,11}=1.796\), \[10.67\pm1.796\frac{15.24}{\sqrt{12}}=\boxed{[2.17,13.57]}.\]

The interval excludes \(0\), so there is evidence of a difference in the population means.

Example 2.3 · One-sample t-test

A new drug is given to 16 people with colds. Their hours of sleep are

\[8.1,6.7,3.3,7.2,8.1,9.2,6.0,7.4,6.4,6.9,7.0,7.8,6.7,7.2,7.6,7.9.\]

The control-group mean is 6.6 hours.

(i) Use the data to set up a 95% confidence interval for the mean sleep time after taking the tablet.

(ii) At the 1% significance level, test whether the drug has an effect on mean sleep time.

Show answer

(i) \(n=16\), \(v=15\), \(\bar x=7.094\), \(s=1.276\), and \(t_{0.975,15}=2.131\): \[\boxed{[6.41,7.77]}.\]

(ii) \(H_0:\mu=6.6\), \(H_1:\mu\ne6.6\). The test statistic is \[|t|=\frac{|7.094-6.6|}{1.276/\sqrt{16}}=1.55.\] Since \(1.55< t_{0.995,15}=2.947\), do not reject \(H_0\). There is insufficient evidence at the 1% level that the drug changes mean sleep time.

3. Paper 4 真题

以下收录 2021–2025 Paper 4 中直接考查 normal or t-distribution inference 的真题；同一题干在不同 variant 中相同的试卷合并标注，所有小问均保留。

2021 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 1

A random sample of 7 observations of a variable \(X\) is \[8.26,\ 7.78,\ 7.92,\ 8.04,\ 8.27,\ 7.95,\ 8.34.\] The population mean of \(X\) is \(\mu\).

(a) Test, at the 10% significance level, the null hypothesis \(\mu=8.22\) against the alternative hypothesis \(\mu\ne8.22\).

(b) State an assumption necessary for the test in part (a) to be valid.

Show answer

(a) \(\bar x=8.08\), \(s=0.202\), \(v=6\) and \[t=\frac{8.08-8.22}{0.202/\sqrt7}=-1.83.\] At 10%, \(t_{0.95,6}=1.943\). Since \(|t|<1.943\), do not reject \(H_0\). There is insufficient evidence that \(\mu\ne8.22\).

(b) The population is normally distributed.

2021 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 4

Random samples of 50 and 60 leaf lengths from regions A and B give \[\sum x=282,\quad\sum x^2=1596,\quad\sum y=328,\quad\sum y^2=1808.\] The population mean lengths are \(\mu_A\) and \(\mu_B\).

Carry out a test at the 5% significance level to test \(H_0:\mu_A=\mu_B\) against \(H_1:\mu_A\ne\mu_B\).

Show answer

(a) Use the large-sample statistic \[z=\frac{\bar x-\bar y}{\sqrt{s_x^2/50+s_y^2/60}}.\] Substitution gives \(z\approx0.86\).

(b) The 5% critical values are \(\pm1.96\). Since \(|z|<1.96\), do not reject \(H_0\); there is insufficient evidence of a difference between the population means.

2022 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 5

Raman assumes male giraffe heights are normally distributed. A random sample of 8 heights, in metres, is \[5.2,\ 5.8,\ 4.9,\ 6.1,\ 5.5,\ 5.9,\ 5.4,\ 5.6.\]

(a) Find a 90% confidence interval for the population mean height.

Raman claims that the population mean height is less than 5.9 metres.

(b) Test at the 2.5% significance level whether this sample provides sufficient evidence to support Raman’s claim.

Show answer

(a) \(\bar x=5.55\), \(s=0.377\), \(v=7\), \(t_{0.95,7}=1.895\): \[\boxed{5.55\pm1.895\frac{0.377}{\sqrt8}=[5.30,5.80]}.\]

(b) \(H_0:\mu=5.9\), \(H_1:\mu<5.9\) and \(t=-2.63\). The lower critical value is \(-t_{0.975,7}=-2.365\). Reject \(H_0\); there is sufficient evidence to support the claim.

2022 May/June · 9231/43 · Question 1

The times taken by members of a large quiz club have a normal distribution with mean \(\mu\). For a random sample of 8 members, \(\bar x=33.8\) and \(\sum(x-\bar x)^2=94.5\).

Find a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\).

Show answer \(s^2=94.5/7\), \(v=7\), \(t_{0.975,7}=2.365\): \[\boxed{33.8\pm2.365\frac{\sqrt{94.5/7}}{\sqrt8}=[30.73,36.87]}.\]

2023 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Questions 1–2

The lengths of leaves are normally distributed with mean \(\mu\). For a random sample of 12 leaves, \(\sum x=91.2\) and \(\sum x^2=695.8\).

(a) Find a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\).

At schools P and Q, independent samples have \(n_P=10\), \(\bar x_P=9.12\), \(s_P^2=2.16\), \(n_Q=12\), \(\sum x_Q=99.6\) and \(\sum(x_Q-\bar x_Q)^2=21.5\). The times are normally distributed with the same population variance.

(b) Test at the 5% significance level whether the population mean time at P is greater than that at Q.

Show answer

(a) Compute \(\bar x=91.2/12\), \(s^2=(695.8-91.2^2/12)/11\), then use \[\bar x\pm t_{0.975,11}\frac{s}{\sqrt{12}}.\]

(b) Use the pooled variance \(s_p^2\) and \(v=20\): \[t=\frac{\bar x_P-\bar x_Q}{s_p\sqrt{1/10+1/12}}.\] Compare with \(t_{0.95,20}=1.725\) and state the contextual conclusion.

2023 October/November · 9231/41 and 9231/43 · Questions 1 and 3

Maya’s previous mean practice run time was 4.22 minutes. A random sample of 10 new times has \(\sum x=42.05\) and \(\sum x^2=176.83\). The new times are normally distributed.

(a) Test at the 5% significance level whether Maya’s mean time has improved.

For 10 people, LDL cholesterol levels at the beginning and end of a three-month exercise period are: \[\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrr}\text{Person}&A&B&C&D&E&F&G&H&I&J\\\hline\text{Beginning}&72&84&120&90&102&135&64&75&80&88\\\text{End}&64&76&105&92&105&115&67&75&75&84\end{array}\]

(b) Test at the 2.5% significance level whether the population mean LDL level has reduced by more than 2 units.

Show answer

(a) \(H_0:\mu=4.22\), \(H_1:\mu<4.22\). Use \(t=(\bar x-4.22)/(s/\sqrt{10})\), \(v=9\), and compare with \(-t_{0.95,9}\). Do not reject \(H_0\); insufficient evidence of improvement.

(b) Let \(d=\text{Beginning}-\text{End}\). Test \(H_0:\mu_d=2\), \(H_1:\mu_d>2\) using \(t=(\bar d-2)/(s_d/\sqrt{10})\), \(v=9\). Compare with \(t_{0.975,9}=2.262\) and state the contextual conclusion.

2023 October/November · 9231/42 · Questions 1 and 5

The lengths of leaves are normally distributed. A random sample is \(3.5,4.2,3.8,5.2,2.9,3.7,4.1,3.2\) cm.

(a) Find a 90% confidence interval for the population mean length.

Two independent samples compare the times taken by machines X and Y. The populations are normally distributed with equal variances.

(b) Carry out a t-test at the 5% significance level to test whether the two population means differ.

Show answer

(a) \(\bar x=3.825\), \(s=0.731\), \(v=7\), \(t_{0.95,7}=1.895\): \[\boxed{3.825\pm1.895\frac{0.731}{\sqrt8}}.\]

(b) Use \(t=(\bar x-\bar y)/(s_p\sqrt{1/n_X+1/n_Y})\), \(v=n_X+n_Y-2\). Reject \(H_0\) only if \(|t|>t_{0.975,v}\).

2024 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 1

The times taken by 14 cycling-club members have a normal distribution with mean \(\mu\). The summary statistics are \(\bar x=42.8\) and \(\sum(x-\bar x)^2=941.5\).

Find a 95% confidence interval for \(\mu\).

Show answer \(s^2=941.5/13\), \(v=13\), \(t_{0.975,13}=2.160\): \[\boxed{42.8\pm2.160\frac{\sqrt{941.5/13}}{\sqrt{14}}}.\]

2024 May/June · 9231/43 · Question 6

A random sample of adult wallaby tail lengths is taken from regions X and Y.

(a) Find a 90% confidence interval for the difference between the population mean lengths.

The population means are \(\mu_X\) and \(\mu_Y\).

(b) Test, at the 10% significance level, \(H_0:\mu_Y-\mu_X=1.1\) against the stated alternative.

Show answer

(a) Use the two-sample interval \((\bar y-\bar x)\pm1.645\sqrt{s_X^2/n_X+s_Y^2/n_Y}\).

(b) Use \(z=((\bar y-\bar x)-1.1)/\sqrt{s_X^2/n_X+s_Y^2/n_Y}\) and compare with the 10% critical value; state the conclusion in terms of \(\mu_Y-\mu_X\).

2024 October/November · 9231/41 and 9231/43 · Questions 1 and 6

(a) Unbiased estimates for the heights of two types of beech tree are given. Find a 95% confidence interval for the difference between the population mean heights.

The wingspans of Monarch butterflies from regions X and Y are normally distributed with equal population variances.

(b) Carry out the required two-sample t-test at the stated significance level.

Show answer

(a) Use \((\bar x_A-\bar x_B)\pm1.96\sqrt{s_A^2/n_A+s_B^2/n_B}\) for the large independent samples.

(b) Use the pooled estimate \(s_p^2\) and \(t=(\bar x-\bar y)/(s_p\sqrt{1/n_X+1/n_Y})\), with \(v=n_X+n_Y-2\); compare with the appropriate two-tailed critical value and give the contextual conclusion.

2025 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 2

Two independent normal samples compare scores from schools X and Y.

(a) Find a 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean scores.

(b) Use the confidence interval to explain why there is insufficient evidence at the 5% significance level for a difference in the population means.

Show answer

(a) Use \((\bar x-\bar y)\pm1.96\sqrt{s_X^2/n_X+s_Y^2/n_Y}\) with the given sample summaries.

(b) The interval contains \(0\), so a zero difference remains plausible; hence there is insufficient evidence at the 5% level.

2025 May/June · 9231/43 · Question 5

A doctor investigates blood-glucose concentration in patients at risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

(a) Use a t-test at the 5% significance level to find the range of values of \(k\) for which the result is significant.

(b) State an assumption necessary for the test in part (a) to be valid.

Show answer

(a) Form \(t=(\bar x-k)/(s/\sqrt n)\) and compare \(|t|\) with \(t_{0.975,n-1}\). Thus the significant range is \[\boxed{|\bar x-k|>t_{0.975,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}}.\]

(b) The population is normally distributed and observations are independent.

2025 October/November · 9231/41 and 9231/43 · Questions 1 and 6

Ten school children estimate the size of an acute angle.

(a) Stating any assumptions, calculate a 95% confidence interval for the population mean angle.

Nine athletes are measured before and after a new training programme.

(b) Carry out a paired t-test at the 5% significance level to test the coach’s belief.

(c) Suggest a reason why the paired t-test may not have been appropriate.

(d) Suggest a suitable alternative test.

Show answer

(a) Use \(\bar x\pm t_{0.975,9}s/\sqrt{10}\), after stating random independent observations and approximate normality.

(b) Form the nine differences and use \(t=\bar d/(s_d/\sqrt9)\), \(v=8\). Compare with \(t_{0.95,8}=1.860\).

(c) The differences may not be normally distributed, especially with a small sample.

(d) Use a paired-sample sign test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test, as appropriate.

2025 October/November · 9231/42 · Questions 2 and 5

The total mass of biscuits in a packet has a normal distribution.

(a) Find a 99% confidence interval for the population mean.

Two independent samples compare the mean tensile strengths of materials X and Y.

(b) Use a t-test at the 5% significance level to test whether there is any difference in the mean tensile strengths.

Show answer

(a) Use \(\bar x\pm t_{0.995,n-1}s/\sqrt n\).

(b) Use the pooled statistic \(t=(\bar x-\bar y)/(s_p\sqrt{1/n_X+1/n_Y})\), \(v=n_X+n_Y-2\), and reject \(H_0\) only when \(|t|>t_{0.975,v}\).
