4.2 Inference using normal and t-distributions

Further Probability & Statistics · confidence intervals · t-tests · paired data

CIE 9231 Paper 4 inference using normal and t-distributions，依据 John du Feu Further Probability & Statistics。
重要本节考纲要求

本章使用样本资料对总体均值作估计和检验，包括 confidence intervals、one-sample t-tests、two-sample tests 和 paired-sample t-tests。答案中的每个小问均按 Show answer 展开，并按 Mark Scheme 分点书写。

1. 核心知识点

先选择正确的检验

情境 使用方法 条件与自由度
一个小样本、总体正态、总体方差未知 one-sample t-test / t confidence interval v=n-1
同一对象的 before/after 或自然配对资料 paired t-test：先对差值 d_i 做单样本 t-test 差值总体正态，v=n-1
两个独立小样本 two-sample pooled t-test 两总体正态、独立、总体方差相同，v=n_1+n_2-2
两个独立大样本 normal (z) procedure 独立随机样本；用样本方差估计标准误

不要把 paired data 当成两组独立资料：配对设计必须先计算每一对的差值。也不要在题目没有给出“equal variances”或相同总体方差条件时，擅自使用 pooled t-test。

样本统计量与标准误

\bar x=\frac{\sum x}{n},\qquad s^2=\frac{\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2/n}{n-1}.

单样本或配对差值的标准误为 \operatorname{se}(\bar x)=\frac{s}{\sqrt n}.

两个独立小样本且方差相同，先计算 pooled variance： s_p^2=\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2+(n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2}, 再使用 \operatorname{se}(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)=s_p\sqrt{\frac1{n_1}+\frac1{n_2}}.

Confidence intervals

A two-sided 100(1-\alpha)\% confidence interval is \text{estimate}\pm(\text{critical value})\times(\text{standard error}).

For a normal population with unknown variance, \bar x\pm t_{1-\alpha/2,v}\frac{s}{\sqrt n},\qquad v=n-1.

For the difference of two independent large-sample means, (\bar x-\bar y)\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{s_x^2}{n_x}+\frac{s_y^2}{n_y}}.

Interpret the interval in context. A two-sided confidence interval that excludes 0 gives evidence, at the corresponding two-sided significance level, that two population means differ. A confidence interval for a single mean that excludes the hypothesised value gives the corresponding two-sided test conclusion.

Hypothesis-test method

  1. Define the population parameter and write H_0 and H_1 in symbols. Use \ne for a two-tailed question; use > or < only when the wording specifies a direction.
  2. State the test statistic, substitute the sample statistics, and give its numerical value.
  3. State degrees of freedom and the critical value, or state a p-value if required.
  4. Compare in the correct direction. Reject H_0 only when the statistic lies in the critical region.
  5. Give a contextual conclusion using “sufficient / insufficient evidence”, at the stated significance level.

For one sample, t=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n},\qquad v=n-1.

For paired data, choose and state a consistent direction, for example d=\text{before}-\text{after}, then test t=\frac{\bar d-\mu_{d,0}}{s_d/\sqrt n},\qquad v=n-1.

For two independent small samples, t=\frac{(\bar x-\bar y)-\delta_0}{s_p\sqrt{1/n_1+1/n_2}},\qquad v=n_1+n_2-2.

For two independent large samples, z=\frac{\bar x-\bar y-(\mu_X-\mu_Y)_0}{\sqrt{s_X^2/n_X+s_Y^2/n_Y}}.

常见失分点

  • “Accept H_0” does not prove H_0; write that there is insufficient evidence against it.
  • A 95% confidence interval uses the two-tailed critical value t_{0.975,v}, not t_{0.95,v}.
  • The normality assumption for a paired test applies to the differences, not necessarily to each original set of measurements.
  • Keep the definition of d consistent: changing from x-y to y-x reverses the sign and the alternative hypothesis.

2. 教材 Examples

以下题目来自 John du Feu, Further Probability & Statistics, Chapter 2，题目保持英文。

Example 2.1

A bus company is about to start a scheduled service between two towns some distance apart. Before deciding on an appropriate timetable, they do nine trial runs to see how long the journey takes. The times, in minutes, are

89,\ 92,\ 95,\ 94,\ 88,\ 90,\ 92,\ 93,\ 91.

(i) Use these data to set up a 95% confidence interval for the mean journey time. You should assume that the journey times are normally distributed.

The company regards its main competition as the railway service, which takes 95 minutes.

(ii) Does your confidence interval provide evidence that the journey time by bus is different from that by train?

Show answer

(i) n=9, v=8, \bar x=91.56 and s=2.297. Using t_{0.975,8}=2.306, 91.56\pm2.306\frac{2.297}{\sqrt9}=\boxed{[89.79,93.33]}.

(ii) 95 is outside the interval. Therefore there is sufficient evidence that the mean bus journey time differs from 95 minutes; the evidence indicates that it is less.

Example 2.4

The manufacturers of a dieting compound claim that the use of their product as part of a calorie-counting diet leads to an average extra weight loss of at least five kilograms in a period of months. An experiment is carried out by a consumers’ group that doubts this claim.

The hypotheses are:

H_0:\text{the mean extra weight loss is }5\text{ kg}, H_1:\text{the mean extra weight loss is less than }5\text{ kg}.

The assumptions are that the weight loss in a period of months from a calorie-counting diet, with or without the dieting compound, is normally distributed and that the addition of the dieting compound to the diet does not affect the variance of this random variable.

Thirty-six dieters used the dieting compound with their diets; their weight losses x_i (i=1,\ldots,36) in kilograms are summarised by \sum_{i=1}^{36}x_i=409.32,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{36}x_i^2=6102.39.

Sixty-two dieters followed the same calorie-counting procedure, but did not use the dieting compound; their weight losses y_j (j=1,\ldots,62) in kilograms are summarised by \sum_{j=1}^{62}y_j=571.64,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{62}y_j^2=5618.40.

Carry out the test at the 5% significance level.

Show answer

The sample statistics are \bar x=11.37,\quad s_x=6.433,\qquad \bar y=9.22,\quad s_y=2.388.

The data give \bar x=11.37,\quad s_x=6.433,\qquad \bar y=9.22,\quad s_y=2.388.

The pooled estimate of the common standard deviation is s=\sqrt{\frac{35(6.433)^2+61(2.388)^2}{36+62-2}}=4.326.

The test statistic is t=\frac{(11.37-9.22)-5}{4.326\sqrt{1/36+1/62}}=-3.144.

There are (36+62-2)=96 degrees of freedom. At the 5% one-tailed level, the critical region is t<-1.661.

Since -3.144<-1.661, reject H_0. There is sufficient evidence that the average extra weight loss is less than five kilograms.

Example 2.2

In an experiment on group behaviour, 12 subjects were each asked to hold one arm out horizontally while supporting a 2 kg weight, under two conditions:

  • while together in a group
  • while alone with the experimenter.

The times, in seconds, for which they were able to support the weight under the two conditions were recorded as follows.

Subject A B C D E F G H I J K L
Group 61 71 72 53 71 43 85 72 82 54 70 73
Alone 43 72 81 35 56 39 63 66 38 60 74 52

Find a 90% confidence interval for the true difference between ‘group’ times and ‘alone’ times. You may assume that the differences are normally distributed. Does your result provide evidence that there is any difference in the times in the population as a whole?

Show answer

The sample comprises the 12 differences. It has \bar d=10.67, s=15.24, and v=12-1=11. With t_{0.95,11}=1.796, 10.67\pm1.796\frac{15.24}{\sqrt{12}}=\boxed{[2.77,18.57]}.

The interval excludes 0, so there is evidence of a difference in the population means.

Example 2.3

Tests are being carried out on a new drug designed to relieve the symptoms of the common cold. One of the tests is to investigate whether the drug has any effect on the number of hours that people sleep.

The drug is given in tablet form one evening to a random sample of 16 people who have colds. The number of hours they sleep may be assumed to be normally distributed and is recorded as follows.

8.1,6.7,3.3,7.2,8.1,9.2,6.0,7.4,6.4,6.9,7.0,7.8,6.7,7.2,7.6,7.9.

There is also a large control group of people who have colds but are not given the drug. The mean number of hours they sleep is 6.6.

(i) Use these data to set up a 95% confidence interval for the mean length of time somebody with a cold sleeps after taking the tablet.

(ii) At the 1% significance level, test whether the drug has an effect on mean sleep time.

Show answer

(i) n=16, v=15, \bar x=7.094, s=1.276, and t_{0.975,15}=2.131: \boxed{[6.41,7.77]}.

(ii) H_0:\mu=6.6, H_1:\mu\ne6.6. The test statistic is |t|=\frac{|7.094-6.6|}{1.276/\sqrt{16}}=1.55. Since 1.55< t_{0.995,15}=2.947, do not reject H_0. There is insufficient evidence at the 1% level that the drug changes mean sleep time.

2021 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 1

A random sample of 7 observations of a variable X is 8.26,\ 7.78,\ 7.92,\ 8.04,\ 8.27,\ 7.95,\ 8.34. The population mean of X is \mu.

(a) Test, at the 10% significance level, the null hypothesis \mu=8.22 against the alternative hypothesis \mu\ne8.22.

(b) State an assumption necessary for the test in part (a) to be valid.

Show answer

(a)

\bar x=\frac{56.56}{7}=8.08, s^2=\frac1{6}\left(457.275-\frac{56.56^2}{7}\right)=0.045033, t=\frac{8.08-8.22}{\sqrt{0.045033/7}}=-1.745.

At the 10% significance level the tabular value is t_{0.90,6}=1.440. Since 1.745>1.440, reject H_0. There is sufficient evidence to suggest that the population mean is less than 8.22.

(b) The population is normally distributed.

2021 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 4

Random samples of 50 and 60 leaf lengths from regions A and B give \sum x=282,\quad\sum x^2=1596,\quad\sum y=328,\quad\sum y^2=1808. The population mean lengths are \mu_A and \mu_B.

Carry out a test at the 5% significance level to test H_0:\mu_A=\mu_B against H_1:\mu_A\ne\mu_B.

Show answer

(a) Use the large-sample statistic z=\frac{\bar x-\bar y}{\sqrt{s_x^2/50+s_y^2/60}}. Substitution gives z\approx0.86.

(b) The 5% critical values are \pm1.96. Since |z|<1.96, do not reject H_0; there is insufficient evidence of a difference between the population means.

Raman assumes male giraffe heights are normally distributed. A random sample of 8 heights, in metres, is 5.2,\ 5.8,\ 4.9,\ 6.1,\ 5.5,\ 5.9,\ 5.4,\ 5.6.

(a) Find a 90% confidence interval for the population mean height.

(b) Test at the 2.5% significance level whether this sample provides sufficient evidence to support Raman’s claim.

\sum x=44.4,\qquad \sum x^2=247.48, s^2=\frac1{7}\left(247.48-\frac{44.4^2}{8}\right)=0.1514, so s=0.389. With t_{0.95,7}=1.895, \boxed{5.55\pm1.895\frac{0.389}{\sqrt8}=[5.29,5.81]}.

(b) H_0:\mu=5.9, H_1:\mu<5.9 and t=\frac{5.55-5.9}{0.389/\sqrt8}=-2.544. The lower critical value is -t_{0.975,7}=-2.365. Since -2.544<-2.365, reject H_0; there is sufficient evidence to support the claim.

The lengths of leaves are normally distributed with mean \mu. For a random sample of 12 leaves, \sum x=91.2 and \sum x^2=695.8.

(a) Find a 95% confidence interval for \mu.

At schools P and Q, independent samples have n_P=10, \bar x_P=9.12, s_P^2=2.16, n_Q=12, \sum x_Q=99.6 and \sum(x_Q-\bar x_Q)^2=21.5. The times are normally distributed with the same population variance.

2025 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 4

A researcher is interested in whether there is a difference between two schools in students’ aptitude for English. She randomly chooses 10 students from school X and 8 students of a similar age from school Y to take a written English test. \sum x=612,\quad\sum x^2=40104,\quad\sum y=444,\quad\sum y^2=27460.

(b) The interval contains 0, so a zero difference remains plausible; hence there is insufficient evidence at the 5% level.

2025 October/November · 9231/42 · Questions 2 and 5

The total mass of biscuits in a packet has a normal distribution.

(a) Find a 99% confidence interval for the population mean.

Two independent samples compare the mean tensile strengths of materials X and Y.

(b) Use a t-test at the 5% significance level to test whether there is any difference in the mean tensile strengths.

Show answer

(a) Use \bar x\pm t_{0.995,n-1}s/\sqrt n.

(b) Use the pooled statistic t=(\bar x-\bar y)/(s_p\sqrt{1/n_X+1/n_Y}), v=n_X+n_Y-2, and reject H_0 only when |t|>t_{0.975,v}.
