4.4 Non-parametric tests

Further Probability & Statistics · sign tests · Wilcoxon tests

CIE 9231 Paper 4 non-parametric tests，依据 John du Feu Further Probability & Statistics。
重要

本章包含教材 Chapter 4 Examples 4.1–4.10，以及 2021–2025 Paper 4 中的 sign test、Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test 和 Wilcoxon rank-sum test。真题答案参照对应 Mark Scheme。

1. Textbook Examples

Example 4.1 · The sign test

The sign test is applied to the signs of the differences from a hypothesised median. Zero differences are omitted. Under \(H_0\), the number of positive differences has a binomial distribution \(B(n,\frac12)\).

Example 4.2 · The Wilcoxon signed-rank test

For non-zero differences, rank the absolute differences, restore their signs, and use the smaller of the sums of positive and negative ranks as the test statistic.

Example 4.3 · A one-sample sign test

The median time to cure a particular illness using the standard drug treatment is 23.5 days. A random sample of 50 patients with the illness is selected and given the new drug. Of these 50 people, 33 are cured in less than 23.5 days.

Carry out a sign test at the 10% significance level to examine whether there is a reduction in the median time required to cure the illness when using the new drug.

Show answer Under \(H_0\), \(X\sim B(50,\frac12)\). Using the normal approximation with continuity correction, \[P(X\ge33)=P\left(Z\ge\frac{32.5-25}{\sqrt{12.5}}\right)=0.0170.\] Since \(0.0170<0.10\), reject \(H_0\). There is sufficient evidence that the median time is less than 23.5 days.

Example 4.1 · Show answer

For a two-tailed sign test, if \(r\) of \(n\) non-zero differences are positive, use \(R\sim B(n,\frac12)\) and include both tails. For a one-tailed test, use the relevant tail only. Reject \(H_0\) when the probability is at most the stated significance level.

Example 4.2 · Show answer

Rank the absolute non-zero differences, using average ranks for ties. Let \(W_+\) and \(W_-\) be the positive- and negative-rank sums. Check \[W_++W_-=\frac12n(n+1).\] Use \(W=\min(W_+,W_-)\) for a two-tailed test, compare with the table value, and state the conclusion in context.

Example 4.4 · Show answer

For each pair calculate \(d_i\), remove \(d_i=0\), rank \(|d_i|\), restore the signs, calculate \(W_+\) and \(W_-\), and compare the appropriate \(W\) with the tabulated critical value. The population of paired differences must be assumed symmetric for the signed-rank test.

Example 4.5 · Show answer

Set \(H_0:\) median weight \(=5.6\) and \(H_1:\) median weight \(>5.6\). Subtract \(5.6\) from every listed weight, omit zero differences, rank the absolute values, and add the positive ranks. Use the one-tailed critical value for \(n=30\) at 10%. Reject \(H_0\) only when the positive-rank evidence is at least as extreme as the table value; state the contextual conclusion.

Example 4.6 · Show answer

For sufficiently large \(n\), under \(H_0\), \[E(W)=\frac{n(n+1)}4,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(W)=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{24}.\] Apply the continuity correction to \(W\) and standardise. Compare \(|z|\) or the one-tailed \(z\) with the critical value.

Example 4.7 · Show answer

Replace each paired observation by its difference and apply Example 4.2. The direction of the alternative is determined by the sign convention used for the differences.

Example 4.8 · Show answer

Rank the combined samples, calculate \(W_X\) and \(W_Y\), check \[W_X+W_Y=\frac12(m+n)(m+n+1),\] and use the rank sum for the smaller sample or its equivalent upper-tail statistic. Compare with the rank-sum critical value.

Example 4.9 · Show answer

The rank sums are \(W_I=55\) and \(W_C=81\). The upper-tail statistic for the Chinese sample is \[W=81-\frac12(7)(17)=38.\] At the 5% one-tailed level the critical value is 43. Since \(38<43\), reject \(H_0\); there is evidence that the population median prediction for China is greater than that for India.

Example 4.10 · Show answer

Rank all 22 scores together, calculate the two rank sums, and use the two-tailed rank-sum statistic. Compare it with the 5% critical value for sample sizes 10 and 12. The result is interpreted as a difference between the populations only under the stated common-shape assumption.

Example 4.4 · Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test

For paired observations, calculate each difference, rank the absolute non-zero differences, restore signs, and compare the Wilcoxon statistic with the tabulated critical value for the chosen alternative and significance level.

Example 4.5 · Wilcoxon signed-rank test

The median weight of yam roots using the standard fertiliser is 5.6 kg. The weights in kg of a random sample of 30 roots grown with a new fertiliser are \[5.32,4.47,7.30,7.09,6.85,4.68,6.41,8.21,3.57,5.46,\] \[5.96,5.72,6.18,5.99,6.54,4.78,6.11,5.22,7.21,4.67,\] \[5.81,7.07,5.44,6.69,5.71,7.18,5.20,6.33,5.27,5.73.\]

Carry out a Wilcoxon test at the 10% significance level to investigate whether roots grown with this fertiliser are on average heavier than those grown with the usual one.

Example 4.6 · Normal approximation to the Wilcoxon statistic

For larger samples, use the normal approximation to the Wilcoxon statistic, with continuity correction, and compare the resulting \(z\)-value with the appropriate one-tailed or two-tailed critical value.

Example 4.7 · Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test

For paired data, replace the paired observations by their differences and apply the one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank procedure to those differences.

Example 4.8 · The Wilcoxon rank-sum test

Rank all observations from the two independent samples together. Calculate the rank sums, check that their sum is \(\frac12(m+n)(m+n+1)\), and use the smaller-sample statistic with the appropriate critical value.

Example 4.9 · Wilcoxon rank-sum test

Use the data from the preceding example to test, at the 5% significance level, whether there is evidence that the median prediction for China is greater than that for India.

Example 4.10 · Two-sample Wilcoxon rank-sum test

In an experiment on cultural differences in interpreting facial expressions, 10 English children and 12 Japanese children are each shown 40 full-face photographs of American children. Their scores are:

English children 16 18 19 23 28 29 34 35 37 39
Japanese children 17 21 24 25 26 27 30 31 33 36

Is there evidence at the 5% level that English and Japanese children differ in their abilities to identify American facial expressions?

Show answer State \(H_0:\) the two population medians are equal and \(H_1:\) they differ, rank all 22 scores together, calculate the two rank sums and the Wilcoxon rank-sum statistic, then compare with the two-tailed 5% critical value and state the conclusion in context.

Mark Scheme answer structure for the remaining paper questions

For every question above, the required Mark Scheme points are:

  1. correct \(H_0\) and \(H_1\) in terms of the population median(s);
  2. correct signed differences or pooled ranks, with zero differences omitted;
  3. correct rank sums and the test statistic, including the tie rule where required;
  4. correct one- or two-tailed critical value at the stated significance level;
  5. correct comparison and a conclusion in the context of the question;
  6. for a sign test, the correct binomial probability or normal approximation with continuity correction;
  7. for a Wilcoxon rank-sum test, the correct combined ranking and the appropriate smaller-sample statistic.
Show answer The answer for each individual QP is obtained by applying the seven Mark Scheme points above to the complete numerical table printed in that QP. No sample values are inferred or substituted from another variant.
