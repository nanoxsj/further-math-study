4.3 Chi-squared tests

Further Probability & Statistics · contingency tables · goodness of fit

CIE 9231 Paper 4 chi-squared tests，依据 John du Feu Further Probability & Statistics。
重要

本章保留教材 Examples 和 Paper 4 真题的原始英文题干；答案按对应 Mark Scheme 分点列出。

1. Core formulae

For a contingency table, \[f_e=\frac{(\text{row total})(\text{column total})}{\text{grand total}},\qquad \chi^2=\sum\frac{(f_o-f_e)^2}{f_e}.\]

For an \(m\times n\) contingency table, \(v=(m-1)(n-1)\). For a goodness-of-fit test, subtract one degree of freedom for each estimated parameter, and combine adjacent classes when an expected frequency is below 5.

2. Textbook Examples

Example 3.1 · The chi-squared test for a contingency table

The management of a cinema takes 150 randomly selected questionnaires and records whether those patrons enjoyed or did not enjoy horror films and action movies.

Observed frequency \(f_o\) Enjoyed horror films Did not enjoy horror films
Enjoyed action movies 51 41
Did not enjoy action movies 15 43

Test whether enjoyment of the two types of film is independent at the 10% significance level.

Show answer The expected frequencies are \(40.48,51.52,25.52,32.48\). \[\chi^2=\frac{(51-40.48)^2}{40.48}+\frac{(41-51.52)^2}{51.52}+\frac{(15-25.52)^2}{25.52}+\frac{(43-32.48)^2}{32.48}=12.626.\] Here \(v=1\) and the 10% critical value is \(2.706\). Since \(12.626>2.706\), reject \(H_0\). Enjoyment of the two types of film is not independent; they are associated.

Example 3.2 · Goodness of fit test for the Poisson distribution

The number of telephone calls made to a counselling service is thought to be modelled by the Poisson distribution. Data are collected on the number of calls received during one-hour periods:

Number of calls per hour 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 Total
Frequency 6 13 26 14 7 4 0 70

Use these data to test at the 5% significance level whether a Poisson model is appropriate.

Show answer The estimate is \(\lambda=155/70=2.214\). Combine the last two classes because expected frequencies must be at least 5. The expected frequencies are \(7.65,16.93,18.74,13.84,7.66,5.18\). Use \[H_0:\text{the number of calls is Poisson},\qquad H_1:\text{it is not Poisson}.\] Compute \(\chi^2=\sum (f_o-f_e)^2/f_e\) with \(v=6-1-1=4\), compare with \(\chi^2_{0.95,4}=9.488\), and state the conclusion in context.

Example 3.3 · Goodness of fit test for the binomial distribution

An egg packaging firm has introduced a new box for its eggs. Each box holds six eggs. Data on the number of eggs marked in 100 boxes are:

Number of marked eggs 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 Total
Number of boxes \(f_o\) 3 3 27 29 10 7 21 100

It is thought that the distribution may be modelled by the binomial distribution. Carry out a test at the 0.5% significance level.

Show answer The mean is \(3.45\), so \(6p=3.45\) and \(p=0.575\). Combine the classes \(0,1\) and \(5,6\) because their expected frequencies are below 5. The test statistic is \(\chi^2=39.74\) with \(v=5\). Since \(39.74>\chi^2_{0.995,5}=16.75\) (or the table value used in the text), reject \(H_0\). The binomial model is not appropriate.

3. Paper 4 past-paper questions

2021 May/June · 9231/41 and 9231/42 · Question 2

A driving school employs four instructors. The numbers passing and failing are:

Instructor A Instructor B Instructor C Instructor D Total
Pass 72 42 52 68 234
Fail 33 34 41 58 166
Total 105 76 93 126 400

Test at the 10% significance level whether success in the driving test is independent of the instructor.

Show answer

(a) \(H_0:\) success is independent of instructor; \(H_1:\) success is not independent.

(b) Calculate each expected frequency by \(f_e=(\text{row total})(\text{column total})/400\).

(c) Calculate \(\chi^2=\sum(f_o-f_e)^2/f_e\), with \(v=(2-1)(4-1)=3\).

(d) Compare with \(\chi^2_{0.90,3}=6.251\) and state the conclusion at 10% in context.

2021 May/June · 9231/43 · Question 5

Chai packs china mugs into cardboard boxes. Chai’s manager suspects that breakages occur at random. The number of breakages in a one-hour period has a mean of 2.4. A sample of observations is given in the original question.

(a) Explain how the information supports the manager’s suspicion.

(b) Use the larger sample to carry out a goodness-of-fit test at the 10% significance level.

Show answer Estimate the Poisson parameter from the sample mean, calculate expected frequencies, combine classes with expected frequency below 5, then calculate \(\chi^2\). Use \(H_0:\) the data follow \(\operatorname{Po}(2.4)\) and \(H_1:\) they do not. Compare with the Mark Scheme critical value using the correct degrees of freedom and give the contextual conclusion.

(b) Carry out a goodness-of-fit test at the 10% significance level to test whether \(f\) is a satisfactory model.

Show answer

(a) Use the sample data and the stated PDF to obtain \(\boxed{a=4.6007}\) and the corresponding \(b\).

(b) Calculate the interval probabilities from the proposed PDF, obtain the expected frequencies, combine classes where necessary, then use \(\chi^2=\sum(f_o-f_e)^2/f_e\). Subtract one degree of freedom for each fitted parameter, compare with the 10% critical value, and state the contextual conclusion.
